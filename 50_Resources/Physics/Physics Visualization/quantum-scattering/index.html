Slide 2 · the framework

All the physics hides in one number: $\delta_\ell(E)$

Asymptotic radial wave:   $u_\ell(r) \;\longrightarrow\; \sin\!\left(kr - \tfrac{\ell\pi}{2} + \delta_\ell(E)\right)$  ·  cross-section $\sigma_\ell(E) = \dfrac{4\pi(2\ell+1)}{k^2}\sin^2\delta_\ell(E)$.

Phase shift $\delta$
What this shows
A free wave (green, dashed) and a wave that has passed through a scatterer (blue). After the interaction, the two differ only by a constant phase $\delta$ — that is the full story of scattering, packaged in one number per partial wave.
§ 1 · partial-wave expansion
Everything computed for the next four slides is $\delta(E)$ or something derived from it. The sign of $\delta$ tells you attraction vs. repulsion; jumps of $\pi$ tell you resonances; the low-$E$ slope tells you the scattering length.

§ 4 · scattering length
The whole low-energy behaviour collapses into one length $a$. Slide $D$ up — as the first bound state is about to appear ($D \approx \pi^2/4$ for unit $W$), $a$ shoots to $+\infty$, flips through $-\infty$, and the next branch starts.

Slide 6 · source: RadSqBarr_Resn.xls

A quasi-bound state inside a shell barrier

§ 5 · shell-barrier resonance
At the resonance $E \approx 1.617$ a half-wavelength fits the inner cavity and inside amplitude surges. Raise $H$ or $B$ and the resonance sharpens — longer lifetime, narrower $\Gamma$. This is the radial analogue of $\alpha$-decay.

Slide 7 · putting it together

One formalism, four faces of $\delta$

Every demo is one slice of the S-matrix $S(E) = e^{2i\delta(E)}$. Its poles classify everything we saw.

Bound state

Pole of $S$ on the positive-imaginary $k$-axis ($E < 0$). Wavefunction is normalisable; $\delta$ jumps by $\pi$ as $E$ rises through threshold.

$k = +i\kappa, \quad E = -\kappa^2$
Resonance

Pole of $S$ in the lower-half $k$-plane ($E = E_0 - i\Gamma/2$). $\delta$ rotates by $\pi$ as $E$ sweeps across $E_0$ — Slides 3 and 6 show this directly.

$E = E_0 - i\Gamma/2$
Virtual state

Pole on the negative-imaginary $k$-axis. Not normalisable, but dominates low-$E$ scattering via a huge negative scattering length.

$k = -i\kappa$
Scattering length

Low-$E$ expansion: $k\cot\delta_0 = -\dfrac{1}{a} + \tfrac{1}{2}\, r_0\, k^2 + \ldots$ Divergent $a$ when a bound / virtual state sits at threshold (Slide 5).

$a = -\lim\limits_{k \to 0} \delta_0 / k$
§ 6 · S-matrix synthesis
Every animation we saw is a projection of the same $S(E)$. Resonances are poles; bound states are poles; virtual states are poles; scattering length is the derivative at threshold.

Slide 8 · sources and method

Where this came from

  • 1. Resonance.xls — Breit-Wigner phase shift, $\ell=2$, $\Gamma=0.15$, $E_0=3$. (Duplicate: Resonance (1).xls)
  • 2. RadSqBarr_Resn.xls — resonance inside a radial square barrier; leapfrog integrator for Schrödinger.
  • 3. RadSqWl_PhaseShift.xls — spherical square well phase shift vs. $D$, $E$, $\ell$.
  • 4. RadSqWl_Scat_Length.xls — scattering length as tangent intercept at $E = 0$.

Method. Leapfrog integration of $u''(x) = \left[\ell(\ell+1)/x^2 + V(x) - E\right] u(x)$, grid $dx = 0.02$, $x_{\max} = 10\text{–}12$. Phase shift extracted from the last zero crossing; scattering length from the outer tangent line. Units $\hbar = 2m = 1$ (same convention as the source spreadsheets).
