## Summary

Notes from an article distilling **Itai Yanai's** advice on giving scientific talks/reports. The central message: a talk exists to **explain**, not to **impress**. Every slide and every element on it should earn its place; if it does not serve the main storyline, it is a distraction that adds cognitive load for the audience.

## Section 1 (partial) — Story first, then slides

Before opening a slide editor, answer three questions:

1. **What is my core conclusion?** (我的核心结论是什么？)
2. **What background does the audience need to understand it?** (听众需要哪些背景才能理解它？)
3. **What can I delete without hurting the main storyline?** (哪些内容可以删掉，而不影响主线？)

> **Story first, then slides.** (先有故事，再有幻灯片。)

## Section 2 — A talk is not about "looking impressive" (报告不是为了"显得厉害")

- Many talks are hard to follow not because the research is too complex, but because the speaker is too eager to show how much they did and how much they know.
- Yanai stresses that the goal of a talk is not to **impress**, but to **explain** — not to make people think you are impressive, but to make one concept, finding, or insight clear.
- This matters especially for **conference talks and interview talks**. We are tempted to cram in every experiment, detail, and difficulty to prove how much work we did. But for the audience, excessive detail is a burden, not a bonus.
- A truly effective talk should leave the audience remembering: **what problem you studied, why it matters, and what you found.**

## Section 3 — More slides does not mean a better talk (幻灯片越多，不代表报告越好)

- Yanai warns that **every additional slide adds new cognitive load** for the audience.
- Therefore every page must have a reason to exist. A simple test:

> **"If I delete this page, does the talk's main storyline still hold?"** (如果删掉这一页，报告主线是否仍然成立？)

- If the answer is "yes, it still holds," the page can probably be cut.
- Likewise, **every element on a slide should be spoken to.** Figures, arrows, text, labels, colors, statistical symbols — anything that appears on the page draws attention. If you are not prepared to explain it, it is a distraction.
- The author also suggests that **slide titles should be a summarizing sentence**, not just a vague word like "Results."
