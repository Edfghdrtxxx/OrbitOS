## TL;DR

"Exposure" in European workplaces does **not** require coffee chats, networking drinks, or being the loudest person in the room. Build visibility through **scalable, leveraged work** — public help, internal workshops, and traveling documents — so your expertise reaches people you've never met.

Anchor anecdote: the most introverted person on the speaker's team made Staff Engineer (the ceiling of the IC track) in a relatively short time without socializing.

## The Three Strategies

### 1. Be the public go-to expert

When a question lands in a team channel inside your domain, **answer in the public channel, not in DMs**.

- **Leverage:** one answer helps many people who had the same question.
- **Association:** over time your name gets bound to the topic. People who never worked with you still know "ask X about this."
- **Efficiency:** improves overall team communication, not just your brand.

### 2. Initiate scalable activities

Run small **internal workshops** on a new technical trend or best practices for a tool — outside any formal project.

- A few hours of prep → a recording + slides that circulate internally for months.
- One-off effort, long-tail exposure across teams.

### 3. Write traveling documents

Produce **guides, playbooks, summaries, internal knowledge-base articles** — and put your name on them with an explicit "reach out with questions" invitation.

- Good docs naturally travel across teams.
- They give strangers a low-pressure, organic reason to contact you.
- Connections form from your work, not from forced networking.

## Core Principle

> Visibility at work is not about being loud. It's about making your work visible in ways that **scale over time**.

All three tactics share the same shape: **one unit of effort → many units of exposure**, asynchronously, without requiring extroversion.
