# Why I Am Leaving China — Motivation Anchor

> [!quote] Anchor statement
> **I want a low-friction, trustworthy environment to live a quiet life in.** A society that follows rules, a job that ends at a sane hour, and systems I can rely on for decades. I have concluded China cannot offer this within my lifetime, so the move is one-way.

Read this when the application grind gets hard, when Japan disappoints, or when someone asks "why not just stay?"

## The honest structure of this decision

This is **push-driven, not pull-driven**. Stated plainly in the interview: *"Japan is one of my most reachable destinations for now. I just want to leave China and live in a developed country."* If China's core problems were fixed, I would likely have stayed — but I judge the fix structurally impossible within my lifetime, which is why "would stay if fixed" and "will essentially never return" are both true.

**One genuine pull factor emerged later (2026-07-08):** the CNS/RIKEN environment offers **broader exposure to the world** — international collaboration, a global research community. This is the first thing in the case I am moving *toward* rather than away from. It doesn't change the push-driven diagnosis, but it means the destination is not purely instrumental.

There was no single trigger event. The decision formed by **accumulation** — a long series of disappointments, no one of them decisive.

## The drivers, ranked

The #1 driver is **personal life quality** (not future children — I don't plan to have kids). It has three facets that are really one demand:

1. **A society that follows rules.** The concrete memories that stick: people cutting in line almost every time I queue; smokers everywhere who refuse to stop when asked. Small violations, constantly, with no enforcement and no social cost.
2. **Time and dignity.** I strictly reject 996. I refuse a life where the default deal is my time and health for the employer's convenience.
3. **Predictability and security.** Trust that healthcare, law, information access, and savings won't arbitrarily fail me over decades.
4. **Freedom from the life-script.** *(Added 2026-07-08.)* I reject the stage-gated life course — a person "supposed to" do X at stage Y, happiness equated with 按部就班, 稳定, and 体制内 (国企/央企) careers. My temperament (newest technologies, unconventional paths) is a permanent mismatch with a script-following environment. **Honest mechanism:** Japan is itself heavily script-bound (新卒一括採用, 年功序列); what emigration actually buys is *exemption* — a foreigner stands outside the local script, and distance removes the family/relative enforcement radius. Outside-the-script and foreigner-ceiling are the same coin; both accepted. Stated modestly: not "Chinese minds are conservative" (population claim, exceeds my evidence) but "the script does not fit me and I want out of its enforcement radius" (unassailable).

Secondary drivers, all present: the academic environment in the domestic system, the information environment (censorship, VPN dependence, narrowing access to the global research community), and general social freedom.

## Facts vs. assessments (labeled honestly)

**Facts (my direct experience):** the queueing and smoking incidents; my own working-culture observations; my daily dependence on VPNs for research access.

**Assessments (my judgment, evidence inseparable across layers):** "There is no fundamental trust in society anymore; the whole society is in a mess." This conclusion is built from personal experience, things witnessed around me, and news/online events combined — I cannot untangle the layers, and I acknowledge the online layer passes through an information diet. I hold the conclusion anyway; the direct-experience layer alone justifies leaving.

**Assumption (unfalsifiable, owned):** the problems are structural and will not be fixed within my working lifetime. This is a bet, not a fact.

## Historical-truth integrity (added 2026-07-08, my words verbatim)

> [!quote] Raw statement — recorded in my own words at my request
> "I think Chinese government is disgusting. From one hand, they disguise the information that Japan has apologized multiple times, and guide people to hate Japan and American. However, they are the two countries who help China the most in decades. This government is ungrateful. From the other hand, they refuse to admit what they've done (The Great Leap — at least 20 million died; The Great Revolution of Culture — a systematic persecution). I feel sick to live in a country like this."

This extends the information-environment driver to its most morally charged form: I cannot stand living inside a curated version of history.

**Fact-check appended (Claude, 2026-07-08) — the qualified versions survive scrutiny:**

- *Japan's apologies:* Real and numerous — landmark: Murayama Statement (1995), reaffirmed by Koizumi (2005). The state narrative doesn't hide them outright; it reframes them as insincere via Yasukuni visits and textbook disputes (which did occur). Accurate charge: selective emphasis, not pure fabrication.
- *Help received:* Japan was China's largest bilateral aid donor — ~3.6 trillion yen ODA (1979–2018, Japan MOFA), financing major infrastructure, conspicuously under-publicized domestically; partly context for reparations China waived in 1972. US engagement enabled WTO accession, educated millions of students, opened the export market. "Gift vs. mutual interest" is arguable; the material contribution is not.
- *Great Leap Forward:* "At least 20 million" is conservative. Scholarly excess-death estimates: ~15–30M (demographic), 36M (Yang Jisheng, *Tombstone*), 45M (Dikötter). Official framing remains "three years of natural disasters."
- *Cultural Revolution — correction:* The 1981 Resolution on Party History *did* formally condemn it as a catastrophe and assigned Mao principal responsibility. The accurate charge is "admitted once, then progressively re-buried" — shrinking public memory, restricted research — not "never admitted."
- *Mirror-image caution:* The corrective to one curated narrative is not the opposite narrative. Japan has its own historical revisionism (a right wing minimizing Nanjing) that I will encounter from the other direction. The prize of emigration is not a land of settled truth but a place where contesting the narrative is permitted.
- *Operational prudence:* until departure, this register of opinion stays in this vault — out of exam essays, application materials, official contexts, and public Chinese platforms.

## Stress-test: known risks of a push-driven move

Push-driven migration has a known failure mode — disappointment when the destination has its own version of the problems. Acknowledged risks in Japan: its own overwork culture exists; a foreigner ceiling in academia and society is real; I chose Japan for reachability, not love.

Mitigating insight from the interview: my #1 need — a rule-following, low-friction daily environment — is precisely the thing Japan is famous for delivering. The "merely reachable" destination happens to fit the actual core need better than the choosing process suggests. If Japan still fails on work culture, the demand travels: the goal is *a developed country with daily trust*, and Japan is the first attempt, not the only permissible one.

## Commitments and boundaries

- **Return condition: essentially never.** Force majeure only.
- **Parents aging in China: not a major constraint** — family situation makes it manageable. (Revisit if circumstances change.)
- **No children planned.** Decisions should be optimized for my own life, not a hypothetical family.
- **Non-negotiable carried into Japan:** no 996-equivalent. Escaping one overwork culture to submit to another would void the entire point of this move.

## External assessment (Claude, 2026-07-08)

Recorded at my request so the note carries a critic, not just an advocate.

**What holds up:** decision formed by accumulation, survived consistency checks, execution unusually aligned with motivation. Knowing the move is push-driven is itself protective — no paradise expectations to shatter.

**Size-mismatch warning:** the cited evidence (queue-cutting, smoking, script pressure) supports "China doesn't fit the life I want" — which is unassailable — but not the totalizing "the whole society is broken." The modest claim is the stronger claim; the indictment invites debate, exceeds the evidence, and feeds confirmation bias once the decision is made.

**Two questions answered too quickly:** (1) *Parents* — "not a major constraint" is today's judgment binding a ~40-year-old self whose parents will be in their 70s; the most commonly underestimated cost of one-way emigration from China. (2) *Citizenship fork* — China does not permit dual nationality, so "essentially never return" eventually forces permanent-foreigner-forever vs. naturalize-and-sever. No decision needed now; the fork should be named.

**Biggest practical risk — DOWNGRADED to residual (2026-07-08):** the no-996 non-negotiable could have collided with Japanese academia, where lab culture varies enormously by PI. Investigated with two sources: (1) direct meetings with Imai/Aoi — recruitment-phase data, systematically rosy, weak alone; (2) account of a graduating senior from the lab — insider at the end of the pipeline, little incentive to sugarcoat, strong source. Both report a relaxed, autonomous environment ("free to do one's own stuff at most times"). Assessment: risk not closed but substantially reduced. Residual action: casually verify with one more *current* (not graduating) student if the opportunity arises, and distinguish autonomy from benign neglect of supervision during the first months.
