## Unified TOEFL Phrase & Model Bank

#flashcards/toefl

### TOEFL-01 · Stating a reason (causal closer)

You've given a preference or claim and need a crisp "why" without stalling
?
Phrase: That is precisely why I… / That is precisely the reason that I…
Model: Face-to-face conversation builds empathy in a way screens never can — that is precisely why I prefer meeting friends in person.
Note: Prefer "precisely why" for speech; "the reason that" is slightly more formal. Avoid "the reason why" (mildly redundant).

### TOEFL-02 · Demanding experience / sheer volume

You've named an academic or project experience and want to spell out why it was difficult
?
Phrase: What made it so demanding was…
Model: What made it so demanding was the sheer volume of material — I had to master three years of content across 6 subjects.

### TOEFL-03 · Opening / buying time

Unexpected opinion question — natural conversational beat to think
?
Phrase: I've never really thought about this before, but off the top of my head…
Model: I have never really thought about this before, but off the top of my head, I'd say doing A matters more than doing B.
Pattern: doing A matters more than doing B

### TOEFL-04 · Stating a reason (formal bridge)

Clear stance marker bridging into your core justification
?
Phrase: The reason I hold this view is that…
Model: The reason I hold this view is that sufficient face-to-face communication is one of the most important things for personal growth.

### TOEFL-05 · Stress management / coping mechanism

Managing daily stress, exercise routine, or mental reset after pressure
?
Phrase: When it comes to managing daily stress, I prioritize... as my primary coping mechanism. Even though..., I hardly ever... because...
Model: When it comes to managing daily stress, I prioritize physical activity as my primary coping mechanism. Even though going to the gym demands extra effort after a long day, I hardly ever skip my evening workout because that physical exertion is how I reset mentally. For instance, last week when I felt overwhelmed by deadlines, a quick 30-minute jog effectively cleared my mind and restored my focus. This transition fosters a sense of accomplishment, which invariably leaves me feeling refreshed and mentally composed for the next day.
Collocations: managing daily stress · primary coping mechanism · demands extra effort · hardly ever skip · evening workout · physical exertion · reset mentally · overwhelmed by deadlines · restored my focus · fosters a sense of accomplishment · mentally composed

### TOEFL-06 · Overcoming difficulty / step at a time

Mid-story low point — nearly gave up — before the turnaround
?
Phrase: Honestly, there were times I wasn't sure I'd make it through…
Model: Honestly, there were times I wasn’t sure I’d make it through, but I forced myself to take it one step at a time, and that’s what got me through.

### TOEFL-07 · Concluding a preference

Wrapping up an answer or landing a preference cleanly
?
Phrase: So all in all, that's basically why I'd rather… than…
Model: So all in all, that’s basically why I’d rather travel with one or two close friends than in a big group.

### TOEFL-08 · Concluding / lesson learned

Personal story conclusion — close on what the experience taught you
?
Phrase: That moment taught me that… — and it's an accomplishment I'm still proud of today.
Model: That moment taught me that disciplined, sustained effort really does pay off - and it’s an accomplishment I’m still proud of today.

### TOEFL-09 · Single most important factor

Singling out the primary driver or decisive factor in a learning process
?
Phrase: The single most important factor in X is Y. / Sufficient, constant exposure to… is the single most important factor in…
Model: Sufficient, constant exposure to native input is the single most important factor in language acquisition.

### TOEFL-10 · Mental well-being

Health, stress balance, or lifestyle priorities collocation
?
Phrase: Maintain one's mental well-being
Model: I try to maintain my mental well-being by limiting late-night screen time and going for a short walk after dinner.

### TOEFL-11 · Simultaneous actions

Multitasking, productivity habits, or dual activities
?
Phrase: Doing A while doing B
Model: I often review vocabulary while commuting — doing one focused task while the other runs in the background helps me stay consistent.

### TOEFL-12 · Technology and attention

Impact of digital devices, social media, and constant screen time
?
Phrase: Technology demands our attention constantly, and what it takes from us is…
Model: Technology demands our attention constantly, and what it takes from us is exactly the thing that keeps us calm.

### TOEFL-13 · Superficial trigger vs real motivation

Academic debate — distinguishing surface causes from core student motivation (e.g. lecture recordings)
?
Phrase: The existence of… is merely a superficial trigger; genuinely motivated students remain committed to…
Model: The existence of recordings is merely a superficial trigger; genuinely motivated students remain committed to attending live sessions regardless of available resources.

### TOEFL-14 · Upgrading value claims

Academic / formal stance — replacing weak "means a lot" phrasing
?
Phrase: Which is extremely valuable in modern education. / which is extremely valuable for…
Model: Such collaborative learning develops critical thinking, which is extremely valuable in modern education.

### TOEFL-15 · Aligning with a perspective

Academic Discussion / debate — agreeing or disagreeing with a named speaker or proposal
?
Phrase: I firmly align with his/her perspective regarding… / I firmly align with [Name]'s perspective in opposing/supporting…
Model: I firmly align with his perspective regarding the implementation of lecture recordings. / I firmly align with Leila's perspective in opposing broad device prohibitions.

### TOEFL-16 · Safety net for emergencies

Academic Discussion — highlighting secondary benefits beyond the primary policy objection
?
Phrase: Beyond mitigating… concerns, … provide an indispensable safety net for…
Model: Beyond mitigating truancy concerns, recordings provide an indispensable safety net for students encountering unforeseen personal emergencies, ensuring no critical learning opportunity is lost.

### TOEFL-17 · Formal approval request

Formal email opening — requesting an instructor's or supervisor's approval
?
Phrase: I am writing to respectfully request your approval for… / of…
Model: Dear Prof. Santos, I am writing to respectfully request your approval for taking an advanced seminar next semester.

### TOEFL-18 · Context setting / Given that

Logistical constraint or premise marker (note: "Given that", never "Give that")
?
Phrase: Given the significant distance between… / Given that…
Model: Given the significant distance between our campus and the research facility, an online meeting would be far more practical.
Note: Always use "Given that…", never "Give that".

### TOEFL-19 · Environmental / scale threat

Formal description — large scale or numbers posing harm/danger
?
Phrase: The sheer volume of… is posing a threat to…
Model: The sheer volume of visitors is posing a threat to this area.

### TOEFL-20 · Narrative: Challenge + structured method

Handling an academic challenge, heavy workload, or transition under pressure
?
Phrase: …was a significant challenge because… I handled this by adopting…
Model: Well, adjusting to high school was a significant challenge because the academic demands were exponentially higher than in middle school. Instead of leisurely preparation, I faced the task of condensing three years of curriculum into a shorter timeframe across multiple subjects. I handled this by adopting rigorous time management strategies, breaking down complex topics into manageable daily goals to ensure steady progress without burning out.
Collocations: significant challenge · academic demands · exponentially higher · condensing three years of curriculum · shorter timeframe · rigorous time management · manageable daily goals · steady progress · burning out

### TOEFL-21 · Narrative: Peer dialogue & deep understanding

Collaborative learning, group study, or benefits of academic discussion
?
Phrase: Engaging in dialogue with peers not only… but also broadens one's intellectual horizon.
Model: Engaging in dialogue with peers not only clarifies confusing concepts but also broadens one's intellectual horizon. During my high school years, our daily discussions transformed isolated facts into a cohesive understanding, significantly boosting my retention rates while fostering a deeper appreciation for diverse viewpoints.
Collocations: engaging in dialogue · clarifies confusing concepts · broadens one's intellectual horizon · isolated facts · cohesive understanding · retention rates · diverse viewpoints

### TOEFL-22 · In-person interaction vs screens

Online vs in-person communication, interpersonal skills, and personal growth
?
Phrase: Authentic interpersonal interaction is indispensable for holistic self-development, as it cultivates… that screens cannot replicate.
Model: Authentic interpersonal interaction is indispensable for holistic self-development, as it cultivates empathy and communication skills that screens cannot replicate.

### TOEFL-23 · Seeking expert guidance

Formal email opening — consulting a professor or advisor for resources/direction
?
Phrase: I am writing to seek your expert guidance regarding resources for…
Model: Dear Dr. Imai, I am writing to seek your expert guidance regarding resources for preparing the entrance examination.

### TOEFL-24 · Narrative: Healthy lifestyle & nutrition

Diet, health habits, or personal wellness routine
?
Phrase: My weekly… consists primarily of… I consider this approach… since…
Model: My weekly diet consists primarily of lean proteins like beef and chicken. I consider this approach quite healthy since protein is indispensable for both physical growth and cognitive function. A balanced intake ensures my body and mind remain in optimal condition.
Collocations: consists primarily of · lean proteins · indispensable for · physical growth · cognitive function · balanced intake · optimal condition

### TOEFL-25 · Schools' societal responsibility

Academic debate — educational institutions guiding students' lifestyle / health choices
?
Phrase: As integral components of societal education, schools must…
Model: As integral components of societal education, schools must guide young people toward better lifestyle choices.

### TOEFL-26 · Research integrity and communication

Campus research ethics, planning, and maintaining participant trust
?
Phrase: Careful planning and transparent communication protect… and strengthen trust in…
Model: Careful planning and transparent communication protect participants and strengthen trust in campus research.

### TOEFL-27 · Concession openers (Hardly ever / Even though)

Softening a claim or setting up a contrast before your main assertion
?
Phrase: Hardly ever… / Even though…
Model: Even though preparing for exams is demanding, I hardly ever skip my morning reading session.

### TOEFL-28 · Conversational opinion markers

Conversational opinion opening — stating a personal perspective naturally
?
Phrase: If you ask me… / Personally, I'd say…
Model: If you ask me, studying in a quiet library is far more productive than working in a noisy cafe.

### TOEFL-29 · Debate: Advertising (Inform vs Manipulate)

Debate prompt — does advertising provide useful info or manipulate consumer behavior?
?
Phrase: While… does offer…, I align more with the view that it manipulates consumer behavior.
Model: While advertising does offer valuable market information, I align more with the view that it manipulates consumer behavior. Marketers strategically employ psychological cues to bypass rational decision-making, triggering impulsive, emotional purchases. Although this ecosystem provides diverse options for customers, the underlying intent often prioritizes profit over genuine consumer needs, subtly steering choices toward what is sold rather than what is needed.
Collocations: valuable market information · align more with the view that · manipulates consumer behavior · strategically employ psychological cues · bypass rational decision-making · impulsive, emotional purchases · prioritizes profit over · genuine consumer needs

### TOEFL-30 · Debate: Trainable mental skills vs innate talent

Debate prompt — is focus under pressure innate or trainable through deliberate practice?
?
Phrase: …are trainable, not fixed at birth. / Claiming "you can't teach it" just ignores how actual high-level training works.
Model: Mental skills like focus under pressure are trainable, not fixed at birth. Sports psych research and every serious golf coach show athletes build clutch performance through deliberate practice, visualization, breathing routines, and exposure. Plenty of pros started shaky under the lights and got better. Claiming "you can't teach it" just ignores how actual high-level training works.
Collocations: focus under pressure · trainable, not fixed at birth · build clutch performance · deliberate practice · under the lights · high-level training

### TOEFL-31 · Accountability / Pay no price for being wrong

Policy / governance debate — decision-makers who bear no consequences for errors
?
Phrase: It is hard to imagine a more stupid or more dangerous way of making decisions than by putting those decisions in the hands of people who pay no price for being wrong.
Model: It is hard to imagine a more stupid or more dangerous way of making decisions than by putting those decisions in the hands of people who pay no price for being wrong.
Collocations: hard to imagine a more… way of · putting decisions in the hands of · pay no price for being wrong · skin in the game

### TOEFL-32 · Narrative: Best recent purchase / AI tool

Useful purchase, technological tool, or something that streamlined your workflow
?
Phrase: The most delightful purchase I've made recently was… What made it such a smart decision was…
Model: The most delightful purchase I've made recently was subscribing to an AI service. What made it such a smart decision was its incredible utility in streamlining my daily workflows. It essentially acts as a second brain, helping me organize my mind and clarify my intentions. Just last week, when I hit a wall analyzing complex experiment data, this tool proved invaluable in helping me move forward efficiently.
Collocations: delightful purchase · smart decision · streamlining my daily workflows · acts as a second brain · clarify my intentions · hit a wall · proved invaluable

### TOEFL-33 · Narrative: In-store vs online shopping

Shopping preference — physical retail vs online shopping; tactile experience
?
Phrase: When it comes to shopping, I firmly prefer the tactile experience of… over…
Model: When it comes to shopping, I firmly prefer the tactile experience of traditional brick-and-mortar stores over the convenience of online platforms. My primary motivation is the ability to physically interact with products, which offers a level of assurance that screens simply cannot replicate. For example, last weekend, visiting a local mall allowed me to compare textures and sizes firsthand, leading to a much more confident purchasing decision.
Collocations: tactile experience · brick-and-mortar stores · primary motivation · physically interact with · level of assurance · cannot replicate · compare textures and sizes firsthand · confident purchasing decision

### TOEFL-34 · Contrast / Concession pivot

Acknowledging the opposing side before returning firmly to your stance
?
Phrase: That said… / I can see both sides, but…
Model: Working from home is convenient. That said, I'd still choose an office, because I focus better when other people are around.

### TOEFL-35 · Surface plausibility hedge

Acknowledging an idea looks plausible on the surface before pointing out flaws
?
Phrase: At first glance, it seems to make some sense.
Model: At first glance, it seems to make some sense — free online courses should democratize education. That said, without structure and accountability, a lot of people never finish, so the real bottleneck is follow-through, not access.

### TOEFL-36 · Narrative: Happy childhood memory

Happy childhood memory, memorable school celebration, or nostalgic event
?
Phrase: One of my happiest childhood memories was… where the air was filled with laughter as…
Model: One of my happiest childhood memories was a classroom party where the air was filled with laughter as we shared a birthday cake and played chasing games. I felt genuinely happy and content. Our teacher, who was equally caught up in the atmosphere, meticulously took a lot of photos and videos, which I still look at today to remember that day.
Collocations: happiest childhood memories · air was filled with laughter · shared a birthday cake · played chasing games · genuinely happy and content · caught up in the atmosphere · meticulously took photos

### TOEFL-37 · AI impact: Building learning momentum

Academic Discussion / Opinion — biggest impact of AI on student learning and perseverance
?
Phrase: I believe the most significant impact of… is that they help… build and sustain their learning momentum.
Model: I believe the most significant impact of AI tools on how students learn is that they help students build and sustain their learning momentum. When students get stuck on a particular question, it can be difficult to find the right answer or a clear explanation on their own. With AI tools, however, they can upload the question and keep asking until they genuinely understand it, instead of giving up after one failed attempt. This means they finish the problem the same evening rather than abandoning it for days. Overall, AI tools matter most because they keep students moving forward when they would otherwise stall out.
Collocations: most significant impact · build and sustain · learning momentum · get stuck on · genuinely understand · giving up after one failed attempt · finish the problem the same evening · stall out

### TOEFL-38 · Polite request for further details

Formal email inquiry — politely requesting additional information or clarification
?
Phrase: I would appreciate it if you could provide further details on this matter.
Model: I am very interested in the research opportunity. I would appreciate it if you could provide further details on this matter.
Note: Use "appreciate it if you could + verb" when the object is an action.

### TOEFL-39 · Narrative: Influential educator / Mentor

Teacher, mentor, or supervisor who made a lasting impact on your development
?
Phrase: The educator who left the deepest impression on me was… That rigorous push transformed my perspective…
Model: The educator who left the deepest impression on me was my graduate supervisor, who insisted I master English proficiency rapidly. That rigorous push transformed my perspective; I now realize that linguistic fluency is indispensable for seamlessly reading academic literature and engaging in high-level discourse with global researchers.
Collocations: left the deepest impression · graduate supervisor · insisted I master · rigorous push · transformed my perspective · linguistic fluency · indispensable for · seamlessly reading academic literature · high-level discourse

### TOEFL-40 · Curriculum debate: Core traditional subjects

Debate prompt — keeping foundational subjects at the core of school curricula
?
Phrase: I strongly advocate for maintaining… at the core of curricula.
Model: I strongly advocate for maintaining traditional subjects like mathematics and literature at the core of curricula.
Collocations: strongly advocate for · maintaining traditional subjects · at the core of curricula

### TOEFL-41 · Narrative: Value of teamwork & collaboration

Teamwork, group collaboration, or working with a project partner
?
Phrase: One instance that really highlighted the value of teamwork for me occurred during…
Model: One instance that really highlighted the value of teamwork for me occurred during a particle physics project. By brainstorming ideas with my partner, we uncovered perspectives I had completely overlooked, which significantly improved the depth and quality of our final presentation.
Collocations: highlighted the value of teamwork · particle physics project · brainstorming ideas · uncovered perspectives · completely overlooked · depth and quality

### TOEFL-42 · Narrative: Big city living / Urban infrastructure

Living location preference — big cities vs small towns; access to healthcare/amenities
?
Phrase: I prefer living in a big city because it offers convenience and access to…
Model: I prefer living in a big city because it offers convenience and access to essential resources. Big cities possess superior infrastructure and a wide array of specialized facilities, all of which enhance overall quality of living. For example, last month when my girlfriend experienced a sudden medical emergency, we were able to reach a top-tier hospital within minutes. This gave us a real sense of security.
Collocations: prefer living in a big city · convenience and access · essential resources · superior infrastructure · a wide array of · specialized facilities · enhance overall quality of living · sudden medical emergency · top-tier hospital · within minutes · real sense of security

### TOEFL-43 · Cultural perspectives in problem-solving

Cross-cultural collaboration, studying abroad, or learning from diverse backgrounds
?
Phrase: It broadened my perspective on how diverse cultures approach problem-solving.
Model: Collaborating with international researchers broadened my perspective on how diverse cultures approach problem-solving, showing me that complex technical issues often benefit from multiple viewpoints.
Collocations: broadened my perspective · diverse cultures · approach problem-solving · multiple viewpoints

### TOEFL-44 · Friendship & emotional comfort

Dealing with adversity, emotional resilience, or the importance of close friends
?
Phrase: When circumstances become difficult, I take comfort in the company of…
Model: When circumstances become difficult, I take comfort in the company of my closest friends. Sharing my thoughts with them helps me recharge and regain emotional balance.
Collocations: circumstances become difficult · take comfort in the company of · closest friends · regain emotional balance

### TOEFL-45 · Email: Rescheduling & preposition rules

Requesting a meeting time change (preposition distinction: "on [Day] at [Time]")
?
Phrase: We would like to request an alternative time slot on [Day] at [Time]. / The time is [Day] at [Time].
Model: We would like to request an alternative time slot on Thursday at 5:00 PM.
Note: Preposition usage: Use "on Thursday at 5:00 PM" when specifying the slot. Contrast: "The time is Thursday at 5:00 PM." (no 'on' directly after 'is').

### TOEFL-46 · Email: Formal academic letter frame

Formal course email salutation and professional student signature block
?
Phrase: Dear Prof. [Name], ... Yours sincerely, [Name], [Course] Student
Model: Dear Prof. Santos,
[Body paragraph...]
Yours sincerely,

Reid Hu
Nuclear Electronics Course Student
Collocations: Dear Prof. · Yours sincerely · Course Student

### TOEFL-47 · Campus policy: Mandatory writing workshops

Advocating for required writing workshops / structured academic skill training
?
Phrase: I strongly advocate mandatory writing workshops every semester because they save students time and provide…
Model: I strongly advocate mandatory writing workshops every semester because they save students time and provide instant feedback from teachers. Specifically speaking, these workshops have experienced instructors and various essential resources that can significantly improve our writing skills. For example, last semester, attending a mandatory writing workshop allowed me to compare my writing with the teacher's feedback immediately, which gave me confidence that other methods cannot simply replicate.
Collocations: strongly advocate · mandatory writing workshops · save students time · provide instant feedback · experienced instructors · essential resources · significantly improve · compare my writing with · gave me confidence · cannot simply replicate

### TOEFL-48 · Debate: Instant feedback vs written comments

Preference for live/instant feedback over delayed written comments
?
Phrase: I strongly support instant feedback rather than written feedback because it saves students time and lets us…
Model: I strongly support instant feedback rather than written feedback because it saves students time and lets us fix mistakes on the spot. Specifically speaking, we can ask the teacher a follow-up question right away instead of waiting days for comments. For example, last semester, getting instant feedback in a writing workshop allowed me to revise my thesis immediately, which gave me clarity that written comments alone could not match.
Collocations: strongly support · instant feedback rather than written feedback · saves students time · fix mistakes on the spot · ask a follow-up question right away · waiting days for comments · revise my thesis immediately · gave me clarity · could not match
