## Format scope

> [!warning] Two Speaking tasks
> 1. **Listen and Repeat** — exact repetition; prefab phrases do not help.
> 2. **Take an Interview** — natural answers about experience/opinion; **all cards below**.
>
> Treat phrases as flexible moves, not rigid scripts. Over-templated delivery can lower the score.
>
> ETS: [Speaking content](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/about/content/speaking.html) · [2026 update](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/upcoming-updates-jan-2026.html)

**Card shape used here** (do not put a bare `?` line in this meta section — only inside Flashcards below):

- Line 1…n: front = **scenario / cue** (what you see when reviewing)
- Next line: only `?` (one-way multi-line separator; `??` would add reverse cards — do not use here)
- Following lines: back = **Phrase + Model** (+ optional Note/Alt) — what you recall aloud
- Then a **blank line** (ends the card)

Creates **one** card per block: **scenario → phrase + model**.

**To add a phrase:** copy a block, keep `?` alone on its line, no blank line inside the block, blank line after the block, new ID in the heading.

**Daily review:** command *Spaced Repetition: Review flashcards* → deck `flashcards/toefl/speaking` (~5/day). Read the scenario, say the phrase aloud in a full sentence, then flip and rate.

**On-demand practice:** *"Generate a Take-an-Interview prompt and a model answer that naturally uses my due collocations."*

## Category scheme

Rhetorical function. ID prefixes: `OPEN` · `OPIN` · `EXMP` · `CONT` · `CONC` · `ACAD`.

## Flashcards

#flashcards/toefl/speaking

### OPEN-01 · Opening / buying time

Unexpected opinion question — natural beat to think
?
Phrase: I've never really thought about this before, but off the top of my head…
Model: I've never really thought about this before, but off the top of my head, I'd say staying in touch with old friends matters more than making new ones.
Pattern: doing A matters more than doing B

### OPIN-01 · Stating an opinion

Interviewer asks what you think or prefer — mark a clear stance, conversationally
?
Phrase: If you ask me… / Personally, I'd say…
Model: If you ask me, group projects teach you more than studying alone, just because you have to explain your ideas out loud.

### OPIN-02 · Stating a reason (causal closer)

You've given a preference or claim and need a crisp "why" without stalling
?
Phrase: That is precisely why I… / That is precisely the reason that I…
Model: Face-to-face conversation builds empathy in a way screens never can — that is precisely why I prefer meeting friends in person.
Note: Prefer "precisely why" for speech; "the reason that" is slightly more formal. Avoid "the reason why" (mildly redundant).

### OPIN-03 · Stating a reason (formal stance)

Clear opinion marker that bridges into the justification — slightly more formal than OPIN-01
?
Phrase: The reason I hold this view is that…
Model: The reason I hold this view is that sufficient face-to-face communication is one of the most important things for personal growth.

### EXMP-01 · Giving a personal example

You've stated an opinion and need concrete support
?
Phrase: A good example from my own experience is that… / is when…
Model: A good example from my own experience is that last month I noticed I'd spent an entire Sunday answering messages and scrolling on my phone, and by evening I felt drained.
Alt: A good example from my own experience is when I joined a study group last year — my grades went up because we kept each other accountable.

### EXMP-02 · Why it was hard

You've named an experience and want to spell out why it was hard or significant
?
Phrase: What made it so demanding was…
Model: What made it so demanding was the sheer volume of material — I had to master three years of content across six subjects.
Alt: What made it so demanding was juggling lab work and coursework at the same time, with neither giving me any slack.

### EXMP-03 · Low point → turnaround

Mid-story low point — nearly gave up — before the turnaround
?
Phrase: Honestly, there were times I wasn't sure I'd make it through…
Model: Honestly, there were times I wasn't sure I'd make it through, but I forced myself to take it one step at a time, and that's what got me through.
<!--SR:!2026-08-01,3,250-->

### EXMP-04 · Mini-story (challenge + method)

Difficult transition or how you handled pressure — ready narrative block
?
Phrase: …was a significant challenge because… I handled this by…
Model: Well, adjusting to high school was a significant challenge because the academic demands were exponentially higher than in middle school. Instead of leisurely preparation, I faced the task of condensing three years of curriculum into a shorter timeframe across multiple subjects. I handled this by adopting rigorous time management strategies, breaking down complex topics into manageable daily goals to ensure steady progress without burning out.

### EXMP-05 · Mini-story (peers / dialogue)

Studying with others, discussion, or how you learn best
?
Phrase: Engaging in dialogue with peers not only… but also…
Model: Engaging in dialogue with peers not only clarifies confusing concepts but also broadens one's intellectual horizon. During my high school years, our daily discussions transformed isolated facts into a cohesive understanding, significantly boosting my retention rates while fostering a deeper appreciation for diverse viewpoints.

### EXMP-06 · Lifestyle / daily-life topic

Habits, health, or daily routine — concrete personal block
?
Phrase: My weekly… consists primarily of… I consider this approach… since…
Model: My weekly diet consists primarily of lean proteins like beef and chicken. I consider this approach quite healthy since protein is indispensable for both physical growth and cognitive function. A balanced intake ensures my body and mind remain in optimal condition.

### CONT-01 · Contrast / concession

Acknowledge a counterpoint, then return to your position
?
Phrase: That said… / I can see both sides, but…
Model: Working from home is convenient. That said, I'd still choose an office, because I focus better when other people are around.
<!--SR:!2026-08-01,2,230-->

### CONT-02 · Concession openers

Soften a claim or set up a contrast before your main point
?
Phrase: Hardly ever… / Even though…
Model: Even though online courses are convenient, I hardly ever skip live discussions — that's where the real learning happens for me.

### CONC-01 · Concluding / wrapping up

Time almost up — land the answer cleanly instead of trailing off
?
Phrase: So all in all, that's basically why I'd rather… than…
Model: So all in all, that's basically why I'd rather travel with one or two close friends than in a big group.

### CONC-02 · Concluding / lesson learned

Personal story done — close on what it taught you
?
Phrase: That moment taught me that… — and it's an accomplishment I'm still proud of today.
Model: That moment taught me that disciplined, sustained effort really does pay off — and it's an accomplishment I'm still proud of today.

### ACAD-01 · Single most important factor

Single out the one factor that matters most among several
?
Phrase: The single most important factor in X is Y. / Sufficient, constant exposure to… is the single most important factor in…
Model: Sufficient, constant exposure to native input is the single most important factor in language acquisition.

### ACAD-02 · Well-being collocation

Health, stress, balance, or lifestyle priorities
?
Phrase: maintain one's mental well-being
Model: I try to maintain my mental well-being by limiting late-night screen time and going for a short walk after dinner.

### ACAD-03 · Simultaneous actions

Doing two things at once (habit, skill, multitasking opinion)
?
Phrase: doing A while doing B
Model: I often review vocabulary while commuting — doing one focused task while the other runs in the background helps me stay consistent.

### ACAD-04 · Technology and attention

Phones, social media, or digital life — ready insight line
?
Phrase: Technology demands our attention constantly…
Model: Technology demands our attention constantly, and what it takes from us is exactly the thing that keeps us calm.

### ACAD-05 · Face-to-face vs screens

Online vs in-person interaction, or holistic development
?
Phrase: Authentic interpersonal interaction is indispensable for…
Model: Authentic interpersonal interaction is indispensable for holistic self-development, as it cultivates empathy and communication skills that screens cannot replicate.
