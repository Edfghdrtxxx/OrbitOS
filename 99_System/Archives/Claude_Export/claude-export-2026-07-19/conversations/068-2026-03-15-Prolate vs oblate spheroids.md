Both terms describe **ellipsoids** — 3D shapes formed by stretching or squishing a sphere — but in opposite directions:

| | **Prolate** | **Oblate** |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | Elongated along one axis (tall & narrow) | Flattened along one axis (short & wide) |
| **Analogy** | Rugby ball / American football | M&M candy / flying saucer |
| **Axis** | Polar axis > equatorial radius | Equatorial radius > polar axis |

A simple way to remember: think of a **sphere being squeezed vs. stretched**:
- **Prolate** → pulled at the poles → cucumber/egg shape
- **Oblate** → squashed at the poles → pancake/lens shape

---

**Examples in physics** (relevant to your research!):

- **Oblate nuclei**: Many atomic nuclei are oblate — flattened like a disk due to rotation. The Earth itself is slightly oblate (bulges at the equator).
- **Prolate nuclei**: Some nuclei are elongated like a football. Deformed nuclei (oblate or prolate) are characterized by their **quadrupole deformation parameter β₂** — positive β₂ = prolate, negative β₂ = oblate.
- **Prolate**: Many elliptical galaxies, certain stars rotating slowly
- **Oblate**: Rapidly spinning stars, gas giant planets (Jupiter, Saturn)

The key parameter is the **aspect ratio** — which axis is longer relative to the other.
