### 2. assistant — 2026-04-01T01:31:59.241950Z

 Yes. **Retain** (verb) → **retention** (noun).

The morphological relationship is straightforward: *retention* is formed from the Latin stem *retent-* (past participial stem of *retinēre*, "to hold back") plus the suffix *-ion*, which is a standard Latin nominalizing suffix. English borrowed *retention* directly from Latin *retentio* / French *rétention*, while *retain* came via Old French *retenir* — both ultimately from the same Latin root *retinēre* (re- + tenēre, "to hold").

This is a common pattern in English where a verb of French/Latin origin has a paired noun drawn more directly from the Latin participial stem, such as:

- *conceive* → *conception*
- *receive* → *reception*
- *perceive* → *perception*
- *maintain* → *maintenance* / *detention* pairs with *detain* similarly

So while the two words look somewhat different on the surface, they are indeed a matched verb–noun pair sharing the same etymological root.
