---

### 1. Idiomatic

**"I'm not sure about** the SI unit of R **you got from** your formula."

---

### 2. Academic / Formal

"I **have doubts regarding** the SI unit of R **derived in** your formula."

> or, more precisely:
> "I **question** the SI unit of R **as derived from** your formula."

---

### 3. Notes

**Shared fixes**

- `I doubt about` → `I'm not sure about` / `I have doubts regarding` — "doubt" as a verb takes a direct object with no preposition (*I doubt X*), or you use the noun form (*have doubts about/regarding X*). "I doubt about" is a classic preposition intrusion error.
- `derived from your formula` → kept in Academic; in Idiomatic, `you got from` is more natural for speech — "derived from" is fine academically but slightly stiff in casual conversation.

**Idiomatic only**

- `I have doubts regarding` → `I'm not sure about` — lighter, more conversational; native speakers rarely say "have doubts" in quick spoken exchanges.

**Academic only**

- `your formula` → consider `the formula` or `the expression above` in a paper, since "your" can sound informal in written academic register.

Still not quite natural. The issue is with **"in terms of"** — this phrase means *"with respect to / when measured by a certain metric"*, e.g., *"in terms of cost, option A is better."* It doesn't collocate well with expressing personal doubt about a specific thing.

The preposition you need after "doubts" is simply **about** or **regarding**:

| Your attempt | Why it's off | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| *doubts in terms of the SI unit* | "in terms of" frames a metric, not an object of doubt | *doubts **about** the SI unit* |

So the clean versions remain:

- **Idiomatic:** *"I'm not sure about the SI unit of R you got from the formula."*
- **Academic:** *"I have doubts **about** / **regarding** the SI unit of R derived from the formula."*

**Rule of thumb:** "In terms of" is a discourse marker for *shifting perspective or criterion*, not a general-purpose preposition. When in doubt, test by substituting *"when it comes to"* — if the sentence still makes sense, "in terms of" works; if it sounds forced, it doesn't belong there.
