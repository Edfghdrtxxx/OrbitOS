## Setup

Two point masses, $m_1$ and $m_2$, connected by massless rigid rods of length $l_1$ and $l_2$. Generalized coordinates: $\theta_1, \theta_2$, both measured from the vertical.

**Cartesian positions:**
$$x_1 = l_1\sin\theta_1, \quad y_1 = -l_1\cos\theta_1$$
$$x_2 = l_1\sin\theta_1 + l_2\sin\theta_2, \quad y_2 = -l_1\cos\theta_1 - l_2\cos\theta_2$$

**Velocities** (differentiate w.r.t. time):
$$\dot x_1 = l_1\dot\theta_1\cos\theta_1,\quad \dot y_1 = l_1\dot\theta_1\sin\theta_1$$
$$\dot x_2 = l_1\dot\theta_1\cos\theta_1 + l_2\dot\theta_2\cos\theta_2,\quad \dot y_2 = l_1\dot\theta_1\sin\theta_1 + l_2\dot\theta_2\sin\theta_2$$

## Kinetic and potential energy

$$v_1^2 = l_1^2\dot\theta_1^2$$
$$v_2^2 = l_1^2\dot\theta_1^2 + l_2^2\dot\theta_2^2 + 2l_1 l_2\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)$$

(the cross term comes from $\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2+\sin\theta_1\sin\theta_2$ — this coupling term is the entire reason the problem isn't separable)

$$T = \tfrac12(m_1+m_2)l_1^2\dot\theta_1^2 + \tfrac12 m_2 l_2^2\dot\theta_2^2 + m_2 l_1 l_2\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)$$

$$V = -(m_1+m_2)g l_1\cos\theta_1 - m_2 g l_2\cos\theta_2$$

## Lagrangian and Euler–Lagrange equations

$$L = T - V$$

Applying $\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \dot\theta_i} - \dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \theta_i} = 0$ for $i=1,2$, and letting $\Delta \equiv \theta_1-\theta_2$, everything collapses (after the product-rule/chain-rule terms cancel nicely) to:

$$(m_1+m_2)l_1\ddot\theta_1 + m_2 l_2\ddot\theta_2\cos\Delta + m_2 l_2\dot\theta_2^2\sin\Delta + (m_1+m_2)g\sin\theta_1 = 0$$

$$l_2\ddot\theta_2 + l_1\ddot\theta_1\cos\Delta - l_1\dot\theta_1^2\sin\Delta + g\sin\theta_2 = 0$$

These are the equations of motion — coupled, second-order, **nonlinear** (through $\sin\Delta$, $\cos\Delta$).

## Solving for the accelerations

Write as a linear system in $(\ddot\theta_1,\ddot\theta_2)$ and invert the 2×2 mass matrix. With $\det = l_1 l_2(m_1+m_2\sin^2\Delta)$:

$$\ddot\theta_1 = \frac{-m_2 l_2\dot\theta_2^2\sin\Delta - (m_1+m_2)g\sin\theta_1 - m_2 l_1\dot\theta_1^2\sin\Delta\cos\Delta + m_2 g\cos\Delta\sin\theta_2}{l_1(m_1+m_2\sin^2\Delta)}$$

$$\ddot\theta_2 = \frac{(m_1+m_2)(l_1\dot\theta_1^2\sin\Delta - g\sin\theta_2) + \cos\Delta\left(m_2 l_2\dot\theta_2^2\sin\Delta + (m_1+m_2)g\sin\theta_1\right)}{l_2(m_1+m_2\sin^2\Delta)}$$

This is a self-contained algebraic derivation from the Lagrangian above (I re-derived it directly rather than pulling from memory — worth cross-checking against a source like Taylor's *Classical Mechanics* if you're putting this in speaker notes).

## Blind spots worth flagging

1. **No closed-form solution.** Unlike the single pendulum (solvable via elliptic integrals), you can't integrate these analytically. You solve numerically — RK4 or a symplectic integrator — treating $(\theta_1,\theta_2,\dot\theta_1,\dot\theta_2)$ as a 4D state vector.
2. **Chaos.** For large-amplitude initial conditions, the system is chaotic — sensitive dependence on initial conditions with a positive Lyapunov exponent. This is *not* true for small oscillations, only large ones. If you're discussing this pedagogically, it's a natural bridge to nonlinear dynamics.
3. **Small-angle limit ≠ the full problem.** Linearizing ($\sin\theta\approx\theta$, $\cos\Delta\approx1$, drop $\dot\theta^2$ terms) gives two coupled linear ODEs with well-defined normal modes — useful as a sanity check on your numerical integrator at small amplitude, but it's a separate calculation, not a step in the exact derivation.
4. **Canonical momenta are not intuitive here.** $p_{\theta_1} = \partial L/\partial\dot\theta_1 = (m_1+m_2)l_1^2\dot\theta_1 + m_2 l_1 l_2\dot\theta_2\cos\Delta$ — it's *not* just "moment of inertia times angular velocity" for pendulum 1 alone, because of the coupling term. Easy to misuse if you move to a Hamiltonian formulation.
5. **Energy conservation as a numerical check.** Since $L$ has no explicit time dependence, $H=T+V$ is conserved — track it in your integrator to catch numerical drift.
