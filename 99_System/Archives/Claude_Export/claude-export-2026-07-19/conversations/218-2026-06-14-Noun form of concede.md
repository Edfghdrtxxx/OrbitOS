### 2. assistant — 2026-06-14T12:13:56.266391Z

 Yes — **concession** is indeed the standard noun form of the verb **concede**.

The derivational chain is straightforward:

- **Verb:** *concede* (from Latin *concēdere* — *con-* "together/completely" + *cēdere* "to yield, go")
- **Noun:** *concession* (from Latin *concessiō*, stem *concessiōn-*, the nominalized form of the past participle *concessus*)

This Latin pattern — verb → past participle → *-iō/-iōnis* noun — is extremely productive in English and gives us many parallel pairs: *permit → permission*, *admit → admission*, *express → expression*, *possess → possession*.

**One nuance worth noting:** the two words don't perfectly overlap in meaning coverage. *Concede* can mean several things (to yield a point, to acknowledge a truth, to grant a privilege), and *concession* tracks all of these senses — but *concession* has also developed an additional, more specialized sense (a commercial stand or stall, as in a "concession stand") that has no corresponding verbal use of *concede*. That sense evolved from the idea of a privilege or right *granted* by an authority.

So: same root, same family — but *concession* has drifted slightly further in its semantic range.
