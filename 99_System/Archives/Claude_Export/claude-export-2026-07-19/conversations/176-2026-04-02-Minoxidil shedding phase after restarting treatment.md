## 1. Will You Experience a Shedding Phase Again?

**Yes, very likely — and your 4-month break is the key reason.**

If you have only taken a short break from Minoxidil (less than two or three months), you might be able to avoid the shedding phase again. However, a break longer than that is likely to trigger it. Since your break was 4 months, you have crossed that threshold.

The mechanism is well understood: Minoxidil shortens the telogen (resting) phase, pushing dormant hairs out so they can re-enter the growth phase sooner than they naturally would. This premature transition can lead to a phenomenon known as telogen effluvium, where increased hair shedding is observed.

The good news is that if Minoxidil worked the first time around, it is most likely that it will work a second time. The shedding you're experiencing now raises no cause for concern — it is actually a sign that the treatment is working, because the shedding is caused by new hair growing through and pushing out old hairs that are in the resting phase.

---

## 2. Will the Shedding Phase Be Shorter This Time?

**This is where honesty is important: there is no strong peer-reviewed evidence directly comparing the duration of a first vs. second shedding phase.**

What the research *does* say about initial shedding duration: A 2025 study (Bi et al., published in PubMed) of 49 patients using topical Minoxidil for 24 weeks found that participants using 5% Minoxidil exhibited increased shedding for 4–8 weeks, while those using 2% exhibited increased shedding for 8–12 weeks.

⚠️ **Assumption to flag:** The idea that a second shedding phase is "shorter" than the first is widely repeated in online forums and some clinic blogs, but no primary clinical study was found in the available sources that directly and quantitatively compares first vs. restart shedding duration. Any claim about it being definitively shorter should be treated as plausible but unverified.

## 3. Are There Side Effects to This Stop-and-Restart Pattern?

There are documented concerns, though direct research on the specific stop-restart cycle pattern is limited.

- **Hair loss beyond your original baseline is possible.** One study found that four in ten men had hair counts that fell below baseline levels after discontinuation of topical Minoxidil. So it is possible to lose more hair than you regained.

- **Hair loss resumes relatively quickly after stopping.** If you stop treatment, you can expect hair shedding to resume within 3 to 4 months, because Minoxidil does not cure the underlying cause of hair loss — it only treats the symptoms.

- **Stopping abruptly can worsen shedding.** A dermatologist perspective notes that "the worst thing you can do is stop abruptly," as this can make the shedding worse.

- **Underlying hair loss conditions are progressive.** Hair loss conditions such as male pattern baldness and female pattern hair loss are progressive. They will keep getting worse without treatment. This means each period off Minoxidil allows underlying loss to advance.

- **Common local side effects** include redness and irritation at the application site, which is often due to a reaction to propylene glycol. Switching to the foam formulation, which does not contain that ingredient, is equally effective and may reduce irritation.
