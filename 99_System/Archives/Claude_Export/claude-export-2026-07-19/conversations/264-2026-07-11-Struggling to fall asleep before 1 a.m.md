## Why it's hard to fall asleep

The most likely explanation for a consistent inability to sleep before ~1 a.m. is a **delayed circadian phase**, not defective sleep. Your suprachiasmatic nucleus (the brain's master clock) is running on a delayed schedule, so the timing of melatonin release, core body temperature fluctuations, and other biological processes all run several hours behind the typical pattern. When most people's internal clock is sending "wind down" signals, yours is still sending alerting signals. Your internal clock is sending alerting signals until late into the night.

Two contributing factors have support in the literature: a longer-than-average intrinsic circadian period (DSPD may be the result of an intrinsic circadian period that is longer than average), and a hypersensitivity to evening light, which may be a predisposing or maintaining factor for the phase delay. There's also a genetic component — variations in habitual bedtime are 40–50% heritable.

**Important caveat (fact vs. assumption):** I can't diagnose you. A true circadian delay looks different from ordinary insomnia — the distinguishing feature is that once you fall asleep, sleep quality is normal. Unless you have other sleep disorders such as sleep apnea or insomnia, you may actually sleep well with DSPS, in terms of duration and quality of sleep. If instead you're lying awake anxious, waking repeatedly, or sleeping poorly once down, that points toward insomnia, which has different treatment. Formal diagnosis uses sleep logs plus actigraphy over ~7 days and a DLMO measurement.

## Does this mean you need less sleep?

**No** — this is the key distinction. A delayed phase shifts *when* you sleep, not *how much* you need. When the person is allowed to sleep at the biologically correct time, sleep duration and architecture are typically normal. So you likely need the same ~7–9 hours as anyone; your window is just shifted later. The health cost you're intuiting is real, but it comes from the *misalignment* with early obligations (forcing a short night), not from needing less sleep.
