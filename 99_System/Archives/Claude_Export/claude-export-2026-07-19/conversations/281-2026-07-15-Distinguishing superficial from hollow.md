### 2. assistant — 2026-07-15T15:11:49.551883Z

**Superficial** = only at the surface; lacking depth. **Hollow** = empty inside; lacking substance or sincerity.

**Superficial**
- Describes attention, knowledge, understanding, or a person's values.
- "A superficial reading of the report" — you got the surface, missed the argument.
- "Superficial wounds" — literal: skin-deep only.
- Judgment: *shallow, didn't go deep enough.*

**Hollow**
- Describes words, promises, victories, laughter, gestures.
- "A hollow apology" — the form is there, the sincerity isn't.
- "A hollow victory" — won on paper, worthless in effect.
- Judgment: *the outside is intact but there's nothing inside.*

**The core contrast**

| | Superficial | Hollow |
|---|---|---|
| Metaphor | thin layer | empty shell |
| Missing | depth | content/sincerity |
| Typical target | understanding, person, analysis | words, promises, achievements |

A *superficial* explanation tells you something true but not enough. A *hollow* explanation tells you nothing at all while sounding like it does.

**Etymology anchor** (Online Etymology Dictionary): *superficial* ← Latin *superficies* "surface" (*super* "above" + *facies* "face"). *Hollow* ← Old English *holh* "hole, cave" — a physical cavity. The metaphors still track their origins.

**Trap**: they rarely swap. "A hollow person" ≠ "a superficial person" — the first suggests emptiness or spiritual void (literary), the second suggests someone who cares about looks and status (common criticism).
