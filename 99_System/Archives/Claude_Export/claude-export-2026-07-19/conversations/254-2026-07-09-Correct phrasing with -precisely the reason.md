### 2. assistant — 2026-07-09T05:23:25.863659Z

Both are grammatical, but they differ slightly.

"That is precisely the reason why..." — acceptable and common in speech. Some style guides consider "the reason why" mildly redundant (since "reason" already implies "why").

"That is precisely the reason..." — cleaner, and preferred in formal writing.

Better alternatives depending on what you want:

"That is precisely **why**..." — most concise and natural. (Drop "the reason" entirely.)
→ *That is precisely why the shell gap appears.*

"That is precisely the reason **that**..." — formal, avoids the redundancy debate.
→ *That is precisely the reason that we observe this effect.*

"That is precisely the reason **for**..." — when followed by a noun.
→ *That is precisely the reason for the discrepancy.*

Quick guide:
- Followed by a **clause** (subject + verb) → use **"why"** alone, or **"the reason that"**: *That is precisely why it works* / *the reason that it works*.
- Followed by a **noun** → use **"the reason for"**: *the reason for the delay*.

So the tightest, safest choice in most cases is simply **"That is precisely why..."**
