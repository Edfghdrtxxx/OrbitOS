Here are the key factors to consider when contemplating a hair transplant:

**Medical Suitability**
- **Cause of hair loss** — Transplants work best for androgenetic alopecia (pattern baldness), not for diffuse or autoimmune-related loss (e.g., alopecia areata)
- **Donor hair availability** — You need sufficient healthy hair at the back/sides of the scalp to harvest. Thin or sparse donor areas limit what's achievable
- **Age** — Younger patients (under ~25–30) are often advised to wait, as their hair loss pattern may still be progressing and a premature transplant can look unnatural later

**Realistic Expectations**
- A transplant redistributes existing hair — it doesn't create new hair. Total hair volume doesn't increase
- Results look natural only when density, hairline design, and direction are done well
- Multiple sessions may be needed for significant coverage

**Technique Choice**
- **FUT (strip method)** — higher graft yield, leaves a linear scar, better for large sessions
- **FUE (individual extraction)** — no linear scar, longer recovery per graft, slightly lower yield but preferred by most patients today
- **DHI/robotic FUE** — variations with different precision/cost tradeoffs

**Surgeon & Clinic Quality**
- This is arguably the most critical factor. Results vary enormously by skill
- Look for board-certified dermatologists or plastic surgeons with a dedicated hair restoration focus
- Review before/after portfolios critically, and seek independent patient reviews

**Cost**
- Costs typically range from **$3,000–$15,000+** depending on graft count, technique, and location
- It's generally not covered by insurance
- Beware of unusually cheap clinics — quality and safety standards vary significantly internationally

**Recovery & Downtime**
- Expect redness, scabbing, and shedding of transplanted hairs for the first 2–4 weeks
- Final results take **12–18 months** to fully appear
- Physical activity restrictions apply for several weeks post-procedure

**Ongoing Hair Loss**
- A transplant doesn't stop native hair from continuing to fall out
- Most surgeons recommend pairing the procedure with medical treatment (minoxidil, finasteride) to preserve surrounding hair
- Future loss may necessitate additional sessions

**Psychological Readiness**
- The "ugly duckling" phase (months 2–4, when transplanted hair sheds before regrowing) can be distressing
- Going in with patience and realistic expectations significantly affects satisfaction

**Alternative Treatments**
- Before committing, consider whether non-surgical options (minoxidil, finasteride, PRP therapy, low-level laser therapy) might be sufficient or should be tried first

The single best starting point is a consultation with a **reputable, specialized hair restoration surgeon** who can assess your donor density, loss pattern, and candidacy honestly.
