Here are the main GRE Physics preparation websites, separated by what they actually offer.

**Official / primary source (start here)**

The most valuable resource, per multiple sources, is ETS's own free practice book. ETS offers free, official practice books for each GRE Subject Test, each containing a full-length test and answer key, test-taking strategies, and scoring information. Direct link: ets.org/gre/test-takers/subject-tests/prepare.html

Note one factual point worth verifying yourself: sources disagree on question count and time. ETS-adjacent and 2026 guides state ~100 questions over 2 hours across 9 content areas, while Kaplan describes approximately 70 five-choice questions in 2 hours. Confirm the current format directly on ets.org before planning.

**Free practice question banks**

- Career Employer — a free GRE Physics practice test with 160 questions (careeremployer.com)
- Yocket — GRE practice tests with an adaptive algorithm, quick solutions, and instant score reports (prep.yocket.com/gre)

**University department guides (well-regarded, free)**

These tend to give the most honest strategic advice:

- Ohio State Physics (physics.osu.edu/physics_gre) — includes retired past tests
- Stewart Physics (stewartphysics.com/phys-gre.html) — notably argues that the published past tests are by far your most valuable preparation resource, and that working many easy problems matters more than a few hard ones
- Harvey Mudd (hmc.edu/physics/students/resources/gre)

**Facts vs. what you should verify**

The topic weightings (Classical Mechanics 20%, E&M 18%, Quantum 12%, etc.) appear consistently across ETS-derived sources and are reliable. The **test dates and fees** in these third-party guides should not be trusted as-is — most are aggregators, and several carry their own "verify at ets.org" disclaimers. Registration deadlines close well before test dates.
