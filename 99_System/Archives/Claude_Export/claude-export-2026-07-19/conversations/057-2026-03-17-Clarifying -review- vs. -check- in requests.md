Yes, "Please review the file" works perfectly and is actually one of the most natural and professional ways to ask someone to check something. In workplace English, when you say "review," the default interpretation is almost always "examine/check carefully," not "write a critique" (like a book review).

That said, if you want to be even more specific about *what kind* of checking you mean, you can adjust slightly:

**If you want them to check for errors or correctness:**
"Please review the file for any errors."
"Could you look over the file?"

**If you want them to read and give feedback:**
"Please review the file and let me know your thoughts."
"Could you review this and share any feedback?"

**If you want a quick, casual check:**
"Could you take a look at this file?"
"Mind giving this a once-over?"

The bare phrase "Please review the file" is slightly ambiguous only in theory — in practice, context almost always makes the meaning clear. If you send it alongside a report draft, people understand you want them to check it. If you send it alongside a contract, they know you want them to examine the terms.

One thing worth noting: "review" carries a connotation of *careful, thorough* examination, whereas "check" can feel lighter and quicker. So "Please review the file" actually sounds more professional and diligent than "Please check the file." Both are correct, but "review" elevates the register a bit.

So short answer — yes, go ahead and use "Please review the file" with confidence. It's clean, professional, and unambiguous in most real-world contexts.
