Here's a combined drill — proofreading habit + the "discuss about" type grammar trap, both built from your actual sample errors above.

**Step 1: The 3-pass proofread (fixes typos like "availibility," "tegother," "dicuss")**
Before submitting anything, do three separate passes — not one combined read:
1. *Content pass* — does the logic/argument make sense?
2. *Word-ending pass* — read each sentence backward, word by word. This forces your eyes off meaning and onto spelling, which is when typos like "colloboration" surface.
3. *Read-aloud pass* — catches missing words and awkward phrasing your eyes skip over.

**Step 2: Transitive verbs that don't take a preposition (fixes "discuss about")**
This is a known category of ESL error where a verb is treated as needing a preposition to link it to its object, when it doesn't. Common ones worth memorizing as a set:

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| discuss about | discuss X |
| explain to me about | explain X to me |
| mention about | mention X |
| contact with | contact X |
| request for | request X |
| enter into (the room) | enter the room |
| approach to | approach X |

**Step 3: Apply it to your own sentences**
Take these two from your samples, close the images, and rewrite them from memory applying both passes:
1. "I politely suggest that we can dicuss about the assignments that each person should complete."
