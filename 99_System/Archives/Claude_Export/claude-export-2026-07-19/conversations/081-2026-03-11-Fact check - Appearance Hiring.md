## Fact Check — Comments on Appearance in Japan

These comments (replying to an original post about "无敌终极奥义寂寞无敌战术") all discuss whether **physical appearance matters in Japan** for academics, hiring, and social life. Here's a breakdown:

---

### Claim 1 — 为你的恨付出我的爱
> "Japanese academic admissions have interviews, and good looks genuinely add points — this is common knowledge."

**Partly misleading.**
- Japanese university admissions are **overwhelmingly exam-driven** (共通テスト/センター試験).
- Some schools include interviews, and implicit attractiveness bias exists as in any country.
- But framing it as an **official or well-established bonus** is an exaggeration. No formal policy exists.

---

### Claim 2 — 她说彩礼750w
> "When grades are similar, it basically comes down to looks."

**Unverified / Anecdotal.** No academic evidence supports this as a systemic practice in Japanese admissions.

---

### Claim 3 — 今夕是何年
> "颜採用" (hired based on looks)

**This term is real.** 「顔採用」(*kao saiyo*) is a documented and discussed phenomenon in Japan, particularly in **service, hospitality, and entertainment industries**. Not universal, but not fabricated.

---

### Claim 4 — skozms
> "Social relationships basically only go by looks, and attitudes toward people are polarized by appearance."

**Overstated, but has a basis.** Attractiveness bias in social dynamics is documented globally, including Japan. The absolute framing ("basically only") is an exaggeration.

---

### Claim 5 — 晨不太晨
> "0 people care about looks."

**Clearly false / sarcastic counter-point.** Research consistently shows appearance affects social and professional interactions worldwide.

---
