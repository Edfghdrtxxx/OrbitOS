### Turn 2 — 2026-08-19 (learner)

> The scope of the time. 3 p.m. is a relatively samll time scope, which is precisely "at" it; while 2020 is a relatively larger time scope, so we could "in" it.

### Tutor

Yes. That is the rule.

Clock time is a **point** — small enough to stand *at*. A year is a **room** — large enough to go *in*. December is the same family as 2020: a container, so *in*.

You have the two ends. Item 2 is still sitting in the middle of your list.

Same scope test: why is it *on Friday*, not *at Friday* or *in Friday*?

### Turn 3 — 2026-08-19 (learner)

> A day is relatively large but not large enough to be deemed as a container. So we are "on" a "platform", like a playground

### Tutor

Yes. Friday is bigger than 3 p.m. and smaller than a month. English treats that middle size as a **surface** you stand *on*, not a room you walk *in*. Your playground image is the right leftover.

So the three sizes:

- **point** (clock time) → *at*
- **surface** (a day / a date) → *on*
- **room** (month, year, longer) → *in*

### Tutor

Yes. Both.

Topic → *on*. Field → *in*. *Climate change* is what the work is about; *nuclear physics* is the room the work sits in.

Your six items are now two jobs, not one pile:
