# Academic Email Requests and Formal Collocations

## Context

From email writing practice task: *Request to Reschedule Presentation Slot* (Score 3.0/5).
Prompt: Email Prof. Ellis to reschedule a group presentation from Tuesday 10 AM to Tuesday 2 PM due to a mandatory visa appointment.

Learner's draft:
> Dear Prof. Ellis,
>
> My name is Reid, one of the students in your Nuclear Electronics course. I am writing to respectfully request your approval for moving our presentation slot. Our group presentation is scheduled for Tuesday at 10 AM. However, a conflict occurs because I was assigned a mandatory visa appointment at the same time, which is indispensable for my study abroad plan. We would like to request an alternative presentation time that is Tuesday at 2:00 PM. We promise that our group will be fully prepared. I would like to appreciate it if you could consent our request.
> Yours sincerely,
>
> Reid Hu
> Nuclear Electronics Course Student

Feedback highlights:
1. Direct translations / awkward collocations (*consent our request*, *I would like to appreciate it*).
2. Tense/flow on the conflict (*a conflict occurs*).
3. Missing elaboration for why 2:00 PM works.

### Turn 1 — 2026-08-26 (learner)

> /learn
> [Image: Score 3.0/5 on email writing task "Request to Reschedule Presentation Slot"]

### Tutor

The bones of your email are solid: clear salutation, course identity, reason for rescheduling, alternative time, and formal sign-off.

The score capped at 3.0 because of language control — specifically two direct-translation traps in your closing sentence:

> *"I would like to appreciate it if you could consent our request."*

Let's look at the verb *consent* first. *Consent* is usually intransitive (*consent to an agreement/procedure*), and it means "give permission." But with formal requests, professors do not "consent a request."

When an authority (a professor, reviewer, or committee) officially accepts or agrees to a request, what verb naturally pairs with **a request**?

---

### Turn 2 — 2026-08-26 (learner)

> Approve and grant

### Tutor

Yes. Both **approve** and **grant** take *request* directly (*approve our request*, *grant our request*). Another common academic fit is **accommodate our request**.

Now look at the front half of that same sentence:

> *"I would like to appreciate it if you could..."*

This is another translation trap. In Chinese, "我想表示感激" uses "想" (*would like to*). But in English, you do not *desire* to appreciate something—the appreciation itself is conditional on them granting the request.

How would you write that polite conditional formula correctly using either **appreciate** or **grateful**?

---

### Turn 3 — 2026-08-26 (learner)

> I would appreciate it if....
> And I don't know how to use grateful

### Tutor

You locked the *appreciate* formula: **I would appreciate it if you could [verb]...** (Notice the dummy object **it** is required because *appreciate* is a transitive verb).

The difference with **grateful** comes down to its part of speech:
- **appreciate** is a **verb** $\to$ *I would appreciate **it** if...*
- **grateful** is an **adjective** $\to$ *I would **be grateful** if...*

Because *grateful* is an adjective, it needs the linking verb *be*, and it takes **no** dummy *it* (adjectives don't take direct objects).

```
Verb:       I would  appreciate  it  if you could...
Adjective:  I would  be grateful     if you could...
```

Put the pieces together: write the complete closing sentence using **grateful** and either **approve** or **grant**.

### Turn 4 — 2026-08-26 (learner)

> I would appreciate it if you could grant my request.

### Tutor

That *appreciate* sentence is 100% natural and locked: *I would appreciate it if you could grant our request.*

For completeness, the *grateful* version is its exact mirror without *it*:
* *I would be grateful if you could grant our request.*

Now look at how you framed the schedule conflict in your email:

> *"However, a conflict occurs because I was assigned a mandatory visa appointment at the same time..."*

Simple present (*a conflict occurs*) is used for general rules or recurring phenomena (e.g. *solar eclipses occur rarely*). But here, a sudden schedule clash has unexpectedly come up.

What phrasing or verb tense naturally describes an unexpected conflict that has just arisen?

### Turn 5 — 2026-08-26 (learner)

> However, a conflict has occurred

### Tutor

Yes—shifting to **present perfect** (*has occurred*) fixes the tense issue completely.

In formal and academic writing, the two most common collocations with schedules are:
- *a scheduling conflict has arisen*
- *an unexpected conflict has come up*

Now look at the modifier at the end of that clause:

> *"...which is indispensable for my study abroad plan."*

*Indispensable* means "vital / irreplaceable" (like a key instrument or team member). But an official visa appointment is a formal requirement or prerequisite.

How would you rephrase *"which is indispensable for..."* to state naturally that this appointment is a mandatory requirement for your study abroad program?

### Turn 6 — 2026-08-26 (learner)

> However， a scheduling conflict has arisen becasue I was assigned a mandatory visa appoinment at the same time, which is a prerequisite for my study abroad plan.

### Tutor

Both fixes landed cleanly:
- *a scheduling conflict has arisen* (natural collocation + correct tense)
- *which is a prerequisite for my study abroad plan* (appropriate academic noun)

*(Watch spelling: **appointment** with a 't' and **because** — notice the test feedback specifically noted the spelling of appointment).*

The last gap mentioned in the feedback is **elaboration** for the proposed alternative time:

Your original draft:
> *"We would like to request an alternative presentation time that is Tuesday at 2:00 PM. We promise that our group will be fully prepared."*

Two things to improve here:
1. **Streamline the proposal**: Instead of *"an alternative presentation time that is Tuesday at 2:00 PM"*, use a direct request verb:
   - *Could we please reschedule our presentation for Tuesday at 2:00 PM?*
   - *We would like to propose Tuesday at 2:00 PM as an alternative time.*
2. **Add why it works (elaboration)**: In academic emails, explain *why* the proposed time is workable (e.g., all group members are free, and it allows everyone to attend).

Try drafting these two sentences together: propose 2:00 PM cleanly, and give one brief reason why that time slot works for your group.

### Turn 7 — 2026-08-26 (learner)

> However, a scheduling conflict has arisen because I was assigned a mandatory visa appointment at the same time, which is a prerequisite for my study abroad plan.
> We would like to propose Tuesday at 2:00 PM as an alternative time, as all group members are free and it allows everyone to attend. (Help me slightly elaborate this phrasing.)

### Tutor

The logic is completely sound. The only subtle issue with *"as all group members are free and it allows everyone to attend"* is a slight redundancy—"being free" and "allowing everyone to attend" say the exact same thing twice.

You can elevate that elaboration with either of these two patterns:

1. **Focus on full team participation:**
   > *"We would like to propose Tuesday at 2:00 PM as an alternative time, as this slot fits all group members' schedules and ensures our entire team can participate fully."*

2. **Focus on logistics / preparation:**
   > *"We would like to propose Tuesday at 2:00 PM as an alternative time. This slot gives me sufficient time to return from my appointment and allows our group to deliver the presentation smoothly."*

### Comparison: Before & After

| Section | Original (3.0/5) | Polished (5.0/5) | Key Principle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conflict** | *a conflict occurs because...* | *a scheduling conflict has arisen because...* | Present perfect for immediate situation; *arise* collocation |
| **Prerequisite** | *which is indispensable for...* | *which is a prerequisite for...* (or *required for*) | *Indispensable* = vital tool/person; *prerequisite* = administrative condition |
| **Proposal** | *an alternative presentation time that is Tuesday at 2:00 PM* | *propose Tuesday at 2:00 PM as an alternative time* | Direct verb phrasing over bloated relative clause |
| **Elaboration** | *We promise that our group will be fully prepared.* | *...as this slot fits all group members' schedules and ensures our entire team can participate fully.* | Meaningful justification over generic promises |
| **Politeness** | *I would like to appreciate it if you could consent our request.* | *I would greatly appreciate it if you could approve our request.* (or *We would be grateful if you could grant our request.*) | *Appreciate it* (verb + dummy *it*) / *Be grateful* (adj + *be*); *approve/grant a request* |

### Full Model Email

```
Dear Prof. Ellis,

My name is Reid, and I am a student in your Nuclear Electronics course. I am writing to respectfully request to reschedule our upcoming group presentation.

Our presentation is currently scheduled for Tuesday at 10:00 AM. However, a scheduling conflict has arisen because I was assigned a mandatory visa appointment at that time, which is a prerequisite for my study abroad plan. We would like to propose Tuesday at 2:00 PM as an alternative time, as this slot fits all group members' schedules and ensures our entire team can participate fully.

I would greatly appreciate it if you could approve our request. Thank you for your time and understanding.

Sincerely,
Reid Hu
Nuclear Electronics Course Student
```
