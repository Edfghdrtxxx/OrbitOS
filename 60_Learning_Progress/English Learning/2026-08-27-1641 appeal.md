# appeal / appeal to

### Tutor

Both are marked **always forget**, so the whole drill leans on one split: **you push a request toward someone** vs **something pulls someone in**.

## Stage 1 · Visual mnemonic

**Etymology:** *appeal* ← Latin *appellare* = **ad-** (toward) + **pellere** (to push / drive).
The root picture is always the same: something is **driven toward** a person.

The storybook split is the thing you keep mixing up:

| Picture | Grammar | Direction | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scholar **pushes** a petition toward the professor | you **appeal to** someone | you → them | call upon / make a request |
| Café **pulls** the scholar in | something **appeals to** you | it → you | attract / please |

Same verb, same *to*. Different who-is-the-magnet.

The **noun** sits on both sides too:

- *file an **appeal*** → the petition (the thing you push)
- *the **appeal** of the café* → the pull (the charm)

If you remember only one line: **find who is being pushed toward.** If *you* are pushing, it is a request. If *you* are the one being pulled, it is attraction.

## Stage 2 · Passage

After the group meeting, Mei sat with her draft and a cold cup of tea. The paper had a quiet **appeal**: the figures were clean, the argument was tight, and even a tired reader might stay with it. Still, one paragraph worried her. She needed extra beam time, so she decided to **appeal to** Professor Imai — not with drama, but with a short note showing why the current run could not finish the PID check. “If the data gap stays,” she wrote, “I will file a formal **appeal** for one extra shift.”

Her labmate laughed. “Don’t make it sound like a court case. Just ask. The extra-shift idea will **appeal to** him if you keep the request small.” Mei nodded. What **appealed** to her was not the extra hours themselves, but the chance to stop guessing. A noisy detector has little **appeal** when you are trying to separate isotopes; a clean, complete sample does.

That evening she reread the email. The tone was calm. The ask was specific. Whether Imai granted the time or not, she had at least driven the request toward the right person — and she had not pretended that the messy plot had any remaining charm.

Q1 and Q2 are the mix-up. Q3 and Q4 are solid.

**Q1 — Imai is being pulled, not asked.**
The labmate is *rejecting* the court-case reading: “Don’t make it a formal appeal. Just ask.” Then the *idea* is the subject: *the extra-shift idea will appeal to him*. A thing as subject + *appeal to* + person = **attract**. The word *request* is only the condition (*keep the request small*), not the job of *appeal to*. Neighbor nouns do not pick the meaning. The **subject** does.

**Q2 — Dropping *to Imai* does not flip the verb into “attract.”**
*Mei appeals to Imai* = Mei pushes a request toward Imai. Correct so far.
*Mei decided to appeal* does **not** mean she attracts him. Attract needs a **thing** as subject (*the idea appeals to him*). If Mei stays the subject, she is still the requester. What goes missing is only the **target** — who she is calling upon. Intransitive *appeal* often means “make a formal challenge,” not “be charming.”

**Q3 — Different job. Correct.**
Paper/detector: *appeal* = a property (pull). *A formal appeal* = the petition (the thing you file). *Formal* is a good flag.

**Q4 — Correct.**
*Appeal to* takes the **person/authority**. *Appeal for* takes the **thing you want**. Extra beam time is the goal, so *for*.

Pattern to keep: **Who is the subject?** Person → request. Thing → attract.

## Stage 3 · Collocation & synonym drill

### A. *appeal to* = attract
- Collocations: *appeal to readers / specialists / me*; *designed to appeal to*; *immediately appeal to*
- Near-synonym: *attract* = pull attention, often physical. *Appeal to* = fit someone’s taste. You cannot say *the idea attracts to me*.

### B. *appeal to* = request / call upon
- Collocations: *appeal to a professor / committee / court*; *appeal to someone’s better nature*
- Near-synonym: *ask* is everyday. *Appeal to* is call upon someone, often because the usual path is not enough.

### C. *appeal for* = the thing you want
- Collocations: *appeal for help / more time / one extra shift*
- Distinction: **to** the person, **for** the goal. Never *appeal to extra beam time*.

### D. Noun *appeal*
- Petition: *file an appeal*; *a formal appeal*; *the appeal was rejected*
- Pull: *lose its appeal*; *the appeal of Tokyo*; *little appeal*
