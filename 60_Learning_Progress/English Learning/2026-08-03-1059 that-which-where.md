### Turn 2 — 2026-08-03 (learner + tutor)

**Learner:** 1 + 2 (choosing *and* commas).

**Tutor:** Good pairing — they share one hinge.

**Hinge: Does the clause *identify which one*, or only *add extra info*?**

| Kind | Job of the clause | Commas? | Preferred word (formal AmE) |
|------|-------------------|---------|-----------------------------|
| **Restrictive** | Picks out *which* thing you mean | **No** commas | **that** (or *which*; *that* is the safe academic habit) |
| **Non-restrictive** | Extra comment about an already-clear noun | **Yes** commas | **which** only — not *that* |

Quick test (say the sentence without the clause):

- If the reader no longer knows *which* thing you mean → restrictive → no commas → *that*
- If the noun is already unique/clear and the clause is bonus detail → non-restrictive → commas → *which*

**Worked parallel (same noun, two jobs):**

1. *The paper **that** I cited first is the hardest to find.*  
   Without the clause: “The paper is the hardest to find” — *which* paper? Clause **identifies**. No commas. *that*.

2. *Paper A, **which** I cited first, is the hardest to find.*  
   Without the clause: still Paper A. Clause is **extra**. Commas. *which*.

**where** sits aside for a moment (place/situation); we’ll lock *that/which* first.

**Question (your turn — one pick):**  
Which option is correct, and why (identify vs extra)?

> (B) My advisor’s office, which is on the third floor, has no windows.

Assume: one advisor, one office everyone already knows about.

**On “A lacks the subject”:** A is grammatical; the subject is already there. In a relative clause, **that / which stands in for the noun** — you do **not** add *it*.

| Full idea (two clauses) | Relative clause (one sentence) |
|-------------------------|--------------------------------|
| My advisor’s office is on the third floor. The office has no windows. | *My advisor’s office, **which** is on the third floor, has no windows.* |
| | *which* = the office (subject of *is*) — no second *it* |

**Next hinge (*where*):** *where* = place (or sometimes situation) **in/at which** something happens. It is not a free swap for *that/which* on every noun.

| Noun type | Natural relative | Awkward / wrong |
|-----------|------------------|-----------------|
| place: *lab, city, university* | *the lab **where** we run the beam* | often fine |
| thing/device: *detector, paper, method* | *the detector **that** is used…* | *the detector where is used…* ✗ |
| situation/case (common in academic English) | *a case **where** the approximation fails* | also *a case **in which**…* |

### Turn 7 — 2026-08-03 (learner + tutor)

**Learner:** C — because we can use *in which* to substitute the space.

**Tutor:** Correct choice. Polish on the reason: *where* ≈ *in which* / *at which* when the head noun is a **place** (here: *universities*). So:

> *universities **where** the research focus is…*  
> = *universities **in which** the research focus is…*

“Substitute the space” is close; more precise: **replace the preposition + which that the place needs** (*in/at* + *which*).

*universities that the research focus is…* ✗ (incomplete relation).

**Boundary (keep *where* from overgrowing):**
- place/situation → *where* (or *in which*) often natural  
- device/method/paper as *thing* → prefer *that* / *which* (or *in which* if needed), not bare *where is used*

**Tutor:**

**Fix — correct.** *that* = subject of *reduces* (= the method).

**Reason — clean up:**
1. *where reduces…* is broken because *where* ≈ *in which* (place/situation adverbial). It does **not** fill the **subject** slot of *reduces*. You still need a subject: *a method where **one** reduces noise* / *a method **in which** noise is reduced* — or simply *a method **that** reduces noise*.
2. Identify vs extra is a **separate** switch (commas / *that* vs *, which*). It is **not** what *where* is for. *where* answers: is the head a place/situation linked by *in/at which*?

**who / whom** (people — same “slot” idea as *that/which*):

| Word | Slot in the relative clause | Example |
|------|----------------------------|---------|
| **who** | **subject** (does the verb) | *the student **who** wrote the paper* |
| **whom** | **object** (receives the verb) or after a preposition | *the advisor **whom** I emailed* / *the person **to whom** I spoke* |
| (*whose*) | possession (bonus third) | *a researcher **whose** paper we cite* |

- Identify vs extra still applies: *students who apply…* (no commas) vs *Dr. Imai, who leads DONUTS,…* (commas).
- Formal AmE keeps *whom* for objects; everyday English often uses *who* for both. Academic writing: prefer *whom* after prepositions (*to whom*, *with whom*); *who* as subject is always safe.
- Things/methods/detectors → *that/which*, **not** *who/whom*.

**Quick test for who vs whom:** replace with *he/she* (→ *who*) or *him/her* (→ *whom*).
