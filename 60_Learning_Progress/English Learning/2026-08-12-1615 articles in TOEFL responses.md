# Articles in TOEFL responses

## Context

Learner missed a TOEFL item (Q4). Prompt:

> "I need to buy a used textbook for economics class. Do you know where I can find one?"

- **Chosen:** You can find one at bookstore in the university district downtown.
- **Correct (Option C):** Try the bulletin board in the student center—people post used books there.
- **Official tip:** Option C is the only response with correct articles and agreement. Distractors omit articles or use the wrong verb form.

This is a **grammar-of-options** trap (articles + subject–verb agreement), not only “which place is more helpful.”

---

### Turn 1 — 2026-08-12 (learner)

> [Image #1] This is a TOEFL listening test and I don't understand.

### Tutor

This item is less about “which place is smarter” and more about **how the options are written**.

The tip is doing the diagnosis for you:

> Option C is the only response with **correct articles** and **agreement**. The distractors **omit articles** or use the **wrong verb form**.

So look at your line:

> You can find one **at bookstore** in the university district downtown.

**Your move:** What’s missing (or wrong) right after `at`?  
Hint: *bookstore* is a countable singular noun here — English almost never leaves that slot empty.

Reply with your fix for that phrase only (one short rewrite). Once that lands, we’ll hit **agreement** (*people post* vs a wrong verb form) and why Option C’s articles (*the* bulletin board, *the* student center) pass.

---

### Turn 2 — 2026-08-12 (learner)

> 1. It is a used textbook, so it is more possibly to find the information at the bulletin board rather than the bookstore.
> 2. It should be at the bookstore.
> 3. I was distracted by the 'bookstore'

### Tutor

You’re reading the item correctly now.

**On (2):** Yes — *at **the** bookstore* (or *at **a** bookstore*). Bare *bookstore* after *at* is the article miss.

**On (3):** That’s the trap. Your eye locked on a plausible place-word and skipped the form. On this item type, an option can *sound* useful and still be wrong because the grammar is broken.

**On (1):** Used books on a bulletin board is good real-world reasoning. Keep it for life. For **this** tip, though, the scorer is mainly checking **articles + agreement**. The bookstore line was already illegal English, so it could never be the key — content didn’t get a fair vote.

**One apply** (new sentence, same hole):

> She left the package at reception in the main lobby.

Rewrite only the bad chunk so the article is right. What do you write?

---

### Turn 3 — 2026-08-12 (learner)

> She left the package at the reception in the main lobby.

### Tutor

**Yes — principle locked.** You filled the empty slot after *at*.

Small native nuance (so the parallel doesn’t mislead you): *at the reception* can sound like a **party**. Clearer AmE / TOEFL form is **at the reception desk**. British English also allows bare *at reception* for the desk-as-function. Your fix still shows you own the rule that mattered on Q4: **don’t leave a bare count place-noun in the option.**

Second half of the tip: **agreement**.

Correct line ends with:

> people **post** used books there.

**Question:** Why *post*, not *posts*? (Name the subject and the rule in one short sentence.)

---

### Turn 4 — 2026-08-12 (learner)

> Because the subject is people which is plural. The verb follows it should reflect that.

### Tutor

**Exactly right.** *People* is plural → present simple uses the base form: **post**, not **posts**.

That’s the second kill-switch on this item type.

### Q4 in one picture

| What pulled you | What actually decides |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| *bookstore* (content lure) | Form of the option |
| — | **Articles:** not *at bookstore* → *at the/a bookstore* |
| — | **Agreement:** *people post*, not *posts* |
| — | Option C: *the* bulletin board, *the* student center + *people post* |

**Exam habit:** if a place-word looks useful, still scan **articles + verb ending** before you pick.

Session closed — both halves of the tip are solid. Optional later: a 3-option mixed drill (articles + agreement only).

## Key rule (locked)

- Countable singular after prep: usually needs **a/the** (*at the bookstore*).
- Campus landmarks often **the**: *the* student center, *the* bulletin board.
- Agreement: plural subject → base verb (*people post*, not *posts*).
- Exam habit: if a place-word looks attractive, still scan articles + verb ending before choosing.
