## Turns

### Turn 1 — 2026-07-26 (learner)

> [Image of AD response] + personal context. How to improve my writing skill?

### Tutor

Your sample is a good place to work from — length is fine (≥100), and you engage both classmates. The lift from ~3/5 to 4–5 on AD is not “write more”; it is **one claim that actually answers the question, then one developed why**.

**What the question is asking**

> What do you think is the **most significant impact** of AI tools on how students learn?

That is a single “most significant,” not a list of good things about AI. Sofia names *faster personalized feedback*; Ethan names *less productive struggle*. A high-scoring post usually **picks a lane** (agree with one, partly with both but still one impact, or a third impact you name clearly).

**What your draft does instead**

| Piece | What it does | Problem for the score |
|-------|----------------|------------------------|
| Opening | “Positive impact… to us” | Aligns with a *side*, not a *specific impact* |
| Point 1 | Practice drop is “superficial” | Reacts to Ethan; still no named impact |
| Point 2 | Tool skill + feedback | New idea (close to Sofia) but abstract |
| Point 3 | “I prefer using AI constantly” | Preference, not impact on *how students learn* |
| Close | “makes learning itself much easier” | First clear impact — but body never built it |

So the reader never gets one through-line. Language nits (`perfer`, `feedbacks`, filler “all in all / basically”) matter later; **structure is the first lever**.

**Target skeleton (10 min AD)**

1. **Claim (1–2 sentences):** Name the impact in plain words. Optionally: “I agree more with Sofia / partly with Ethan, but…”
2. **Why + mini-example (most of the post):** One reason, with a concrete scene (what the student does → what changes).
3. **Close (1 sentence):** Restate the same impact — no “all in all / basically / that’s why.”

Example of a *claim only* (not your full rewrite):

> The most significant impact is that students get usable feedback in minutes instead of days, so they revise while the idea is still in their head.

That sentence already answers the professor. Your opening never does that job.

**Question:** In **one sentence**, what do *you* think the most significant impact is in this thread — Sofia’s feedback speed, Ethan’s lost practice, or a third impact you want to own? Write only the claim sentence (no reasons yet).

### Turn 2 — 2026-07-27 (learner)

> The most significant impact is that AIs have the capability of helping students construct their learning momentum more easily.

### Tutor

That does the job: **one impact**, not a list. “Learning momentum” is your third path — fine for AD as long as the body makes it concrete.

Light language polish (keep your idea; tighten the shell):

| Your claim | Why it weakens slightly | Tighter version of the *same* idea |
|------------|-------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| “AIs have the capability of helping” | Wordy; “capability of” adds nothing | “AI tools help students…” |
| “construct their learning momentum” | Abstract metaphor — reader must guess the picture | Keep the metaphor, but the *why* must show what momentum looks like |

You do **not** need a perfect claim before writing the body. The claim’s job is to name the impact; the body’s job is to make a stranger see it.

**Next piece — why + mini-example (the 3/5 → 4+ lever)**

Abstract: *helps construct learning momentum*  
Concrete pattern: **stuck student → AI action → what changes so they keep going**

Parallel (not your essay — shape only):

> When I get stuck on a proof, I used to stop for a day. Now I ask the model one clarifying question, get unstuck in five minutes, and finish the problem set the same evening. That is momentum: the work does not die between sessions.

Notice: one scene, one causal chain, still about *how students learn* — not “I prefer AI.”

**Question:** In **3–5 sentences**, write only the *why* for *your* claim. One mini-example (what the student does → what the AI does → how that builds momentum). Do not open a second impact; do not write the closing yet.

### Turn 3 — 2026-07-27 (learner)

> For example, when students are confused on a particular question, it is harder for them to seek for the correct answer and vivid explanation in the absence of AIs. While with AIs, they can upload the question and ask till they genuinely understand the question, which encourages students to study, and builds the learning momentum.

### Tutor

This is a real *why*: **stuck → AI action → keep going**. One impact only — good.

**What already works**
- Concrete enough for AD: confused on a question → upload → ask until understand
- Ends by naming *your* claim again (momentum), not a new idea

**What to tighten next time (one layer, not a full rewrite)**

| Your line | Issue | Tighter same idea |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|
| “seek for the correct answer” | *seek for* is non-idiomatic | “find the right answer” / “get a clear explanation” |
| “in the absence of AIs” | stiff; “AIs” as plural agents | “without AI tools” |
| “which encourages… and builds the learning momentum” | *asserts* momentum rather than *showing* it | one time-based detail: finish same day / don’t drop the set for a week |

Showing momentum (optional upgrade, same scene):

> …they can keep asking until the step makes sense, then finish the set the same evening instead of quitting for days.

That is the difference between “sounds like a reason” and “the rater sees the impact.”

You already have enough body for a full post. Last piece of the skeleton:

**Close (1 sentence):** Restate the same impact. No “all in all / basically / that’s why I think.”

Weak (your original style):  
> So, all in all, that’s basically why… learning itself much easier.

Strong (matches *your* claim):  
> Overall, AI tools matter most because they help students keep learning momentum when they get stuck.

**Question:** Write **one closing sentence** for this post — same impact (learning momentum), no filler, no new reason.
