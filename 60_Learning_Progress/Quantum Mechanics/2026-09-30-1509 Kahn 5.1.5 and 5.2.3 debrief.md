**What $\langle T\rangle$ and $\langle V\rangle$ are.** The same sandwich you built on 9/27, with the kinetic and potential pieces of $\hat H$ put in the middle: $\langle T\rangle=\langle\psi|\frac{\hat p^2}{2m}|\psi\rangle$ and $\langle V\rangle=\langle\psi|\frac12m\omega^2\hat x^2|\psi\rangle$. **What the virial theorem says.** In a bound state that doesn't change in time (a classical orbit averaged over a period, or a quantum energy eigenstate), energy has no reason to pile up in $T$ or in $V$ on average, and the split is fixed by the power of the potential: $2\langle T\rangle=k\langle V\rangle$ for $V\propto r^k$. For the GRE, memorize that one line plus its two cases (right panel):

- **Oscillator, $k=2$:** half and half, $\langle T\rangle=\langle V\rangle=\frac12E_n$. This is the one for 5.2.3 Q1.
- **Coulomb/gravity, $k=-1$:** $E=-\langle T\rangle$, $\langle V\rangle=2E$. Hydrogen ground state: $E=-13.6\,\mathrm{eV}$, so kinetic $+13.6$, potential $-27.2$. Same rule for satellites: a lower orbit has more negative $E$ but *more* kinetic energy, so it moves faster. Both come up on the GRE.

One caution: it is a statement about eigenstates. For a superposition you may use it term by term only when the cross terms vanish, which is what the ladder picture in Turn 5 checked.

**Why $\hat p\propto a^\dagger-a$.** You already locked this on 7/26 ([[2026-07-26-2113 Ladder operators]]). $a$ and $a^\dagger$ are built as $a\propto\hat x+\frac{i\hat p}{m\omega}$ and $a^\dagger\propto\hat x-\frac{i\hat p}{m\omega}$. Add them and $\hat p$ cancels: $\hat x\propto a+a^\dagger$. Subtract them and $\hat x$ cancels: $\hat p\propto i(a^\dagger-a)$.

**Memorize for the GRE?** Only the shape, not the constants: $\hat x$ and $\hat p$ are each "one step up plus one step down". That alone gives the Turn 5 ladder picture: one $\hat x$ or $\hat p$ changes $n$ by $\pm1$, a square changes it by $\pm2$ or $0$. It also gives free zeros, like $\langle n|\hat x|n\rangle=0$. The prefactor $\sqrt{\hbar m\omega/2}$ is rarely needed, and when it is, the virial route usually avoids it.
