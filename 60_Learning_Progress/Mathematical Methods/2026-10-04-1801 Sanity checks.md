### Turn 5 — 2026-10-04 (learner)

> half of d and sin theta = d/(2L)

### Tutor

Right. Force balance on one ball gives $\tan\theta=\dfrac{F}{Mg}=\dfrac{kq^2}{Mg\,d^2}$, and for small angles $\tan\theta\approx\sin\theta=\dfrac{d}{2L}$. Setting them equal: $d^3=\dfrac{2kq^2L}{Mg}$. The $2$ in the correct option is your $\dfrac{d}{2}$. Your picked option is what $\sin\theta=\dfrac{d}{L}$ gives.

So Q4 and Q5 are one slip seen twice. The formula wants one length (charge-to-charge separation, ball-to-centreline distance) and the problem hands you a different one ($d$ to the plane, $d$ between the balls). The fix is not a check at the end; it is one line at the start: write the formula's length in terms of the given length ($r=2d$, $x=\frac d2$) before substituting.
