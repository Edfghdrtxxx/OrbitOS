# MATE pad threshold and hit time

Question from the research seats: for the MATE TPC (multi-purpose active-target time projection chamber, Institute of Modern Physics, Lanzhou; readout by GET, General Electronics for TPCs), what must be true before a pad stores charge and time?

- (a) the event trigger
- (b) the per-pad threshold, in keV or as a multiple of the baseline-noise RMS (root mean square); if only ADC (analog-to-digital converter) counts exist, also the baseline RMS in ADC counts and any ADC-to-energy calibration
- (c) the hit-time method: leading edge, constant fraction, peak, or a waveform fit

Two detector versions appear in the sources. **pMATE** is the 1024-channel prototype with rectangular pads. **MATE-4000** is the later detector with about 3800 triangular pads. The seats' letter names MATE-4000. The machine-learning paper on 12C+12C describes the 1024-channel detector.

## Facts, then inferences

Facts, each quoted above:

- Three published energy numbers exist for the **1024-channel prototype**, and they are not the same quantity or the same run. Zhang et al. 2021 report that a per-pad readout-energy threshold of about 2 keV, and a total-energy threshold of about 150 keV, were achieved. Wang et al. 2022 state a detection threshold of around 50 keV on each pad for the 12C+12C measurement. J. L. Zhang et al. 2026 state that the pad threshold was set to 0.12 MeV (120 keV) for the 19O+12C measurement.
- Those papers also state the event trigger. Wang et al. 2022: anticoincidence of a downstream silicon detector and the GEM, with the GET trigger rate limited to 500 Hz. J. L. Zhang et al. 2026: GEM signal, vetoed by six beam-path pads and by the GET busy signal; GEM rate 1.5–3.3 kHz, accepted trigger rate 70–200 Hz.
- No sentence in those PDFs, in the arXiv machine-learning paper, in the ChinaXiv abstracts of Li 2024 and Nan 2024, or in the MATE-4000 annual-report extract gives a MATE threshold in units of baseline RMS, a baseline RMS in ADC counts, or an ADC-to-energy calibration.
- No sentence in those MATE sources names leading edge, constant fraction, peak time, or a waveform fit as the **experimental** hit time. The arXiv simulation sentence says the simulated pulses go through "peak analysis and threshold filtering." It gives no threshold value.
- The MATE-4000 annual-report extract says the triangular pad plane is connected to GET. It gives no threshold and no hit-time method. The printed active volume is 30 mm × 30 mm × 20 mm. Collaboration slides from 2025, which are not a paper, say 30 × 30 × 20 cm³ and about 3800 channels (`https://indico.impcas.ac.cn/event/172/contributions/1151/attachments/566/1396/202509_NingtaoZhang_Huizhou.pdf`). Both wordings are quoted. The unit is unresolved.
- The vendored digitizer keeps every pad with charge greater than zero and does not apply `fThreshold = 80`. The project noise notes add 0.027 MeV of Gaussian noise to every pad.

Inferences, labeled as such:

- The 2 keV sentence is a demonstrated sensitivity in the 2021 commissioning ("were achieved"). The 50 keV and 0.12 MeV sentences are the thresholds of later runs ("is around", "was set to"). A gain "optimized for fusion-residue and α particle detection" (Wang et al. 2022) can move the effective energy threshold between runs. Treating 2 keV, 50 keV, and 120 keV as one hardware constant is not supported.
- GET, on the evidence of the ACTAR slides, records a channel through a per-channel discriminator after the event trigger. That is standard GET architecture. It does not tell us the MATE discriminator level or whether MATE analysis takes the time from the leading edge, a constant fraction, the sample peak, or a fit.
- `fThreshold = 80` has no unit in the source. It is unused. It is not evidence of an 80 ADC or 80 keV operating point.

## Strongest answer, safest answer, recommendation

**Strongest, and only for the 1024-channel prototype in the 12C+12C dataset.** A pad is stored when two conditions hold. The GET event trigger accepts the event: in that run, an anticoincidence of the downstream silicon and the GEM, with the trigger rate limited to 500 Hz (Wang et al. 2022). The pad's detected energy is above a threshold of around 50 keV (same paper). The hit-time method is not stated. The simulation description says peak analysis plus threshold filtering, without a number (arXiv:2605.28296).

**Safest, for the seats' MATE-4000 question.** No source gives a MATE-4000 per-pad threshold, a noise RMS, an ADC calibration, or a hit-time method. The published keV numbers belong to the 1024-channel rectangular prototype, and they span 2 keV, 50 keV, and 120 keV. The images now in use are the 80×48 triangular plane. Copying any of those three numbers onto that plane would invent a readout.

**Recommendation: the safest answer.** Keep the charge-threshold proxy that is already declared, and do not call a new sample a measured readout. Reason: the fidelity rule in the task is that every model input must be obtainable from a real TPC readout. The literature supplies neither one MATE-4000 threshold nor a hit-time method. Among the prototype numbers, 50 keV is the operating threshold written for the 12C+12C pMATE run, and 0.12 MeV is the operating threshold written for a later pMATE run. Neither is a MATE-4000 specification, and neither comes with the time algorithm. The 2 keV figure is a sensitivity that was achieved on the prototype, not the threshold those later runs say they set.

If a later note needs a GET-wide practice statement, the supported statement is this. An external or detector trigger opens the event. Each AGET channel has a discriminator, and the charge range, peaking time, and sampling frequency are configuration choices (Giovinazzo 2017 slides, ACTAR). ACTAR analysis has used both constant-fraction timing and a fit to the reconstructed samples (same slides, slide 14). MATE has not published which of those it uses.
